## Why floor42 local arithmetic can apply later

The inherited master identity and parameterized local theorem explicitly fix
`n>=N`: `T:paper_local/local_comparison.tex:40–97`. The actual count and marginal
stay fixed. A nearest-count positive bracket licenses a mass lower bound; a
nonnegative repair licenses replacing `1/n` by `1/N`. In the whole-count form,
the event bracket must be negative and the total inverse-index correction
nonnegative. These signs are essential, not cosmetic guards.

The finite42 implementations retain them:

* CENTRAL/LEFT MID check a positive cubic raw bound, clamp the repair by
  `max(0,...)`, and require positive event bracket and reserve:
  `central42_v2/accept_mid42_phase_v2.py:27–54` and
  `left42_final/accept_mid42.py:27–61`. The negative-phase improvement also
  requires `D²<42v` and `0<=eta<42` at lines43–49 of the latter.
* LEFT TINY allows the cubic lower bound to be negative. It uses the upper
  score `.4226` in that case, checks positive lower and upper multipliers,
  and clamps the final repair: `normalization_piecewise42_v2.py:33–41,71–89,
  133–146`. The derivation is `P/signed_cubic_repair.tex:70–132` for arbitrary
  `n>=N`; lines135–155 distinguish the finite application. Its fixed acceptor
  still verifies the actual source `N=42,L=48` and `.4226`:
  `left42_tiny_final/accept_tiny42_actual.py:27–52`.
* CENTRAL LOW checks negative event, positive inverse-index correction and
  strict score at both noise endpoints: `central42_v2/central42_kernel.py:
  180–189`; LEFT LOW does so at `left42_final/central42_kernel.py:180–205`.
  The signed Gaussian coefficients are nonnegative, of the form `A+B/lambda`.
  Convexity of the final upper bound and monotonicity of the correction then
  license the whole interval (`P/local42_signed_gaussian_v2.tex:92–119`).
* The joint resource is not inferred from separate ceilings. The proof
  `P/joint_resource.tex:170–234` uses `nD<=N*d_D`, a nonnegative binary penalty,
  positive coefficients and a same-law moment inequality. It derives
  `d/d_*+lambda/Lambda<=1` for all `n>=N`, including explicit zero-residual
  alternatives. This licenses the noise-band gap reduction and outward
  enlargement of both intercepts.
* Central LOW's cache omits the gap parameter. Its reuse is conservative only
  because noise bands increase, the joint bound makes `c=1-d_L` increase,
  and the cached nonnegative numerators are c-independent. The exact
  denominator argument is `P/local42_improvements.tex:209–216`. Reordering
  bands or copying the cache to a different resource is not covered.

TINY rescales to arbitrarily small positive noise; no fixed positive noise
cutoff replaces that limit. At zero noise, `E U²=0` implies `U=0` almost surely;
the all-parameter strict Bernoulli theorem remains a separate analytic input.
See `T:paper_local/local_comparison.tex:318–374,659–669` and
`P/cutoff44.tex:167–176,208–214`. These arguments do not remove that theorem.

## The duplicate LOW evaluations and their actual limitation

`B:finite61/finite61_worker.py:65–78` selects the row and calls
`contract.selected(row,sign,H_cells,outer_phase_cells)` followed by
`kernel.evaluate(cfg)`, then serializes the result. It adds no distinct
mathematical inequality. The full binder runs exactly that evaluation again:
central `bind_low42.py:34–45`, left `bind_low42.py:34–48`.
The fresh inventory checked all152 configurations: central row0/minus uses
32x16, other central rows16x8, all left rows8x4. Both native task definitions
require `--replay`. The binder additionally checks complete ordered coverage,
contracts, row hashes, counts, reserves, equality with the freshly evaluated
result and its negative controls. These additional checks must be retained.

The historic coverage counts in `P/cutoff44.tex:284–290` are19,842,356 central
LOW gates and3,671,330 left LOW gates. Thus one duplicated complete evaluation
set corresponds to23,513,686 recorded strict gates. This is a source-reported
work count, not a measured runtime or a fresh gate PASS.

Under a reviewed replacement execution design, one could either (a) run
unchanged full binders against independently pinned stored witnesses, or
(b) retain fresh producers and replace the second arithmetic pass by a binder
that authenticates the complete computed outputs and preserves all original
non-arithmetic checks/controls. Option(b) changes acceptance semantics and
needs adversarial review; this report implements neither option. Source-only
B contains neither original LOW indices nor reference row JSONs, so option(a)
is currently blocked on obtaining those inputs. B's manifest deliberately
requires their fresh generation. Current `finite61_batch.py:57–84,101–103`
requires all fresh row receipts and reconstructed original index hashes.
Current public `replay.py:104–117` requires all152+31 actions. Omitting
producers or disabling replay in that protocol would not be a completed run.

## Central-strip regression is not literal certificate equivalence

The inherited `T:baseline/DEPENDENCY_MAP.md:66–70` calls the central strip job
an extra regression because support acceptance verifies the same strip cells.
The conclusion may be redundant for the selected central route, but the
literal equality claim is inaccurate:

* `T:old/root/accept_support47.py:19–24,46–56` checks10 radius rows and45
  exterior rows, requiring only a negative contact bound; its p cover is
  `[.38,.47]`. The separate strip acceptor checks47 rows on `[.38,.474]`,
  a bound below `-.097`, adjacency/cardinality1503, and a false narrower-strip
  control (`accept_panel_exterior47.py:15–25,28–64`).
* The two exterior tables differ already at the first row: support has
  rho1.1495, theta.0153, H1.2565; the separate strip table has rho1.153579,
  theta.016176, H1.292409. The45 support rows do not outward-dominate all the
  older47 premises. The proof text explicitly distinguishes the47-row1503-cell
  calculation from the45-row1431-cell calculation
  (`root/panel_exterior_proof.tex:62–94`).
* The selected central input accepts45 rows and excludes the `.47..48` prep
  panel (`entry/accept_central47.py:26–47,48–81`), explaining why the extra
  two strip rows may be unused in that route. That is route-specific
  redundancy, not identical complete obligations. The old strip job remains
  required by the present tail wrapper. No removal is made here.
