\noindent This research note proves a conditional improvement to the
coarse-entry estimate. Its purpose is to retain information already paid
for in that computation. It does not prove the remaining confinement,
count comparison, or finite base. AI assisted its derivation, implementation
and exposition; the accompanying independent checks have the specific
scope described below.

\section{Why the threshold matters}
The coarse entry used $|H|$ to obtain one bound valid for both signs of the
raw sum threshold. This was convenient for acquiring moments, but loses
information needed at the next step. A term $KH$ can help when $H>0$
and hurt when $H<0$. Replacing it by $|K|\,|H|$ too early discards that help.
We retain its sign within each previously accepted moment cell.

Fix $n\ge N=42$ and $c=\CE$. Let $P$ be an attained fixed-target maximizing
witness with nonnegative excess
\[
 a=\sqrt n\,\{\mathbb P(S_n\le H)-\Phi(H/\sqrt n)\}-c\rho\ge0,
 \qquad \rho=\E|X|^3.
\]
Retain the predecessor, contact, gap, and Fourier hypotheses of
\texttt{INITIAL42\_FIXED\_TARGET\_COARSE\_V7}.
Write $\gamma=\E X^3$, $\beta=\E[X|X|]$ and
\[
 D=(3+\sqrt{10})\rho-\gamma-3J,\qquad
 \mathcal U=(n+H^2)a+n\phi(0)D/6+n^{3/2}L\ge0.
\]
Here $J$ is the actual weighted gap sum and $L$ is the nonnegative
leakage from the conservation identity. Neither may be replaced by the
corresponding quantity of a two-point approximation.

\section{A signed bound from one accepted cell}
The fourth signed pass of the entry proof already gives, on each cell,
\begin{equation}\label{raw}
 \mathcal R_c\le K_F+cA+(cK_1+\varphi W)H+s|H|
       -(c\rho+\varphi\gamma/2)H^2,
 \qquad \varphi=\phi(H/\sqrt n).
\end{equation}
The intervals for $K_1,W,\rho,\gamma$ and $\varphi$ refer to the same law.
The letter $W$ here denotes the signed moment expression in the
remainder; it is not a conditional noise variable.
The cell supplies $\rho\ge r_l$, $\gamma\ge g_l$,
$\varphi\in[\varphi_l,2/5]$, and a previously proved raw ceiling
$\mathcal R_c\le T<Nc_-r_l$, where $c_-\le c\le c_+$.

There are two ordered uses of the raw estimate. The density floor in
\eqref{raw} uses the preceding pass. The newly obtained ceiling $T$ is
then used in conservation to pay for an additional quadratic term.
In detail, put $w=H^2/(2n)$ and $M=c\rho-\phi(0)D/6$.
The conserved inequality gives $e^{-w}M\ge c\rho-T/n>0$ and
\[
 \mathcal R_c-\mathcal U=n(1-e^{-w})M
 \ge \frac{H^2}{2}(c\rho-T/n)\ge\eta H^2,
 \qquad
 \eta=\frac{c_-r_l-T/N}{2}>0.
\]
We used $e^w-1\ge w$. This argument does not use its conclusion to
justify its own density floor.

For $t=c_-$ or $c_+$, define
\[
 B_t=K_F+tA,\quad
 Q_t=tr_l+\min_{\varphi\in[\varphi_l,2/5]}\varphi g_l/2+\eta,
\]
\[
 F_{t,+}=\max(tK_1+\varphi W)+s,\qquad
 F_{t,-}=-\min(tK_1+\varphi W)+s.
\]
The maxima and minima range over the recorded rectangular intervals.
They can be evaluated at the eight corners, since the expression is
affine in each variable separately. All exported $Q_t$ are positive.

\begin{proposition}[Signed resource]\label{signed}
For $h=|H|$ and the original sign $\epsilon$ of $H$,
\[
 0\le\mathcal U\le
 \min\left\{T,\ 
 \max_{t\in\{c_-,c_+\}}
       (B_t+F_{t,\epsilon}h-Q_th^2)\right\}.
\]
At $H=0$ either sign gives the same bound.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For either fixed sign, the maximum defining $F_{t,\epsilon}$ is a
maximum of affine functions of $t$, hence is convex in $t$.
For fixed $h\ge0$, $B_t+F_{t,\epsilon}h-Q_th^2$ is therefore convex.
Its maximum on the enclosing interval for $c$ occurs at an endpoint.
Combining \eqref{raw} with the ordered phase price proves the quadratic
bound. Also $\mathcal U\le\mathcal R_c\le T$.
A negative signed slope remains negative; replacing it by zero would
lose a valid improvement.
\end{proof}

\section{Using a noise restriction without fixing the index}
Let $p=\mathbb P(X>0)$, $q=1-p$, $v=pq$, and
$m=\E X_+=\E X_->0$. The possible atom at zero is assigned to the
nonpositive class. Conditional centering gives
\[
 \theta=1-\frac{m^2}{v},\qquad
 \ell^2=\frac{1-\theta}{v},\qquad
 \lambda=\frac{n\theta}{\ell^2}
          =\frac{nv\theta}{1-\theta}.
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{identities}
 \theta=\frac{\lambda}{nv+\lambda},\qquad
 m^2=\frac{v^2}{v+\lambda/n}.
\end{equation}
The two unnormalized conditional variance contributions $\theta_+,\theta_-$
are nonnegative and sum to $\theta$. Thus
\[
 \beta=(1-\theta)(1-2p)+\theta_+-\theta_-,
 \qquad
 1-2p-2q\theta\le\beta\le1-2p+2p\theta.
\]
These formulas retain the orientation when $p>1/2$.

\begin{lemma}[A necessary filter]\label{filter}
Let $p\in[l,u]\subset(0,1)$ and $0\le\lambda\le\Lambda$.
Put $v_-=\min\{l(1-l),u(1-u)\}$ and let $v_+$ be the maximum of
these two values and $1/4$ if $[l,u]$ contains $1/2$.
For every $n\ge42$,
\[
 \theta\le\Theta=\frac{\Lambda}{42v_-+\Lambda},\qquad
 \sqrt{\frac{v_-^2}{v_-+\Lambda/42}}\le m\le\sqrt{v_+},
\]
\[
 1-2u-2(1-l)\Theta\le\beta\le1-2l+2u\Theta .
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The function $v^2/(v+s)$ increases with $v>0$ and decreases with $s\ge0$.
Apply this fact to \eqref{identities}, then enlarge the coefficients in
the two beta inequalities outward on $[l,u]$.
This includes the limit $n\to\infty$; it is not a fixed-index approximation.
\end{proof}

For a closed region $p\in[l,u]$, $h\in[h_0,h_1]$, $\lambda\in[0,\Lambda]$,
retain every cell meeting these necessary ranges and with its acquired
phase ceiling at least $h_0$. Closed overlaps are retained.
For each retained cell clip $h_1$ by that phase ceiling.
A concave quadratic $B+Fh-Qh^2$, $Q>0$, has its maximum at
\[
 h=\max\{h_0,\min(h_1,F/(2Q))\}.
\]
Apply this to both target endpoints, intersect with that cell's $T$,
and take the maximum over all retained cells.
An empty cell list excludes the region only when the complete ancestor
cover has first been established. A negative resulting upper bound
contradicts $\mathcal U\ge0$.

\section{What is accepted, and what remains}
The export adds three reversible insertions to the pinned signed kernel:
initialization, an append after each surviving cell, and a return field.
It changes none of the old bounds or rejection branches.
Its exact reverse recovers the original source.
The producer replays the final pass from the accepted third-pass rows,
checks every resulting row against the selected entry object, and records
all active cells. It does not claim to repeat the accepted prefix.

The separate acceptance program pins its sources and inherited inputs
before executing them, replays all 2,048 boxes, and compares the complete
ordered JSON object with the proposed export. Comparison uses serialized
types, so an integer cannot be replaced by a Boolean or a floating value.
Duplicate keys, missing, repeated or reordered cells, false bounds and
extra fields are rejected. This proves agreement with the reviewed
deterministic computation, not the correctness of its analytic premises.

The independent mathematical review reconstructs all 1,364 endpoint
quadratics by a different rectangular-support formula, checks every phase
price and preceding density floor, and evaluates all nine recorded closed
regions by endpoints and interior vertices. It imports no inherited
kernel. A separate executable review addresses source binding and coverage.
The original selected entry replay, contact/conservation arguments,
support acquisition, and complete Fourier estimates remain dependencies.

Keeping these signed envelopes requires no new Fourier integration beyond
the last accepted pass. It removes the need to replace that pass by a
single worst-case resource in these downstream estimates. It does not
remove a complete confinement job, count theorem, finite certificate,
or any existing repository proof object.
