\begin{abstract}
The fixed-target formulation removes the need to estimate an unknown
extremal score before using it as a coefficient. We now close a further
initialization gap: a coarse cover of the third-moment interval suffices
to obtain an actual central gap in the support. The main new estimate
bounds a signed fourth-moment charge using signwise Cauchy--Schwarz.
We give the derivation, the scalar acceptance rule, and the precise
remaining dependencies. The resulting acquisition is conditional on the
retained complete Fourier bounds; it is not a proof of the sharp
Berry--Esseen conjecture or of the final local exclusion.
\end{abstract}

\section{The point of the reduction}
Fix $c=C_{\mathrm E}=(3+\sqrt{10})/(6\sqrt{2\pi})$. For a centered,
variance-one law $P$, put $\rho=\E|X|^3$ and maximize the excess
\[
 a=\sqrt n\{F_{P^{*n}}(H)-\Phi(H/\sqrt n)\}-c\rho.
\]
Here $H$ is a raw sum threshold. At a least failing index, the earlier
fixed-target argument supplies an attained witness with $a\ge0$ and
sharp predecessor bounds. The contact coefficient is the known number
$c$, even though the witness's ratio score equals $c+a/\rho$.
This distinction removes the score-cap feedback from the initial entry.
It does not remove attainment, contact, or the analytic Fourier error.

The earlier entry establishes
\[
 n\ge42,\qquad 1\le\rho\le7361/4000,\qquad |X|\le96/25.
\]
The support and moment statements require the fixed-target contact
argument and the stated external structural inequality. They are premises
of this note. The earlier 24-cell calculation obtained a first density
license but left five cells outside the sufficient $D<4$ regime.
The improvement below closes that obstacle on the whole interval and,
more significantly, obtains actual support geometry before using any
central-gap refinement.

Write $\sigma=\sqrt{10}$ and let $J$ be the actual weighted gap sum.
The physical defect and conserved resource are
\[
 D=(3+\sigma)\rho-\gamma-3J\ge0,\qquad
 U=(n+H^2)a+\frac{n\phi(0)D}{6}+n^{3/2}\mathcal L,
\]
where $\gamma=\E X^3$ and $\mathcal L\ge0$ is the contact loss.
The raw remainder $R_c$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{v6:identity}
 R_c=U+n(1-e^{-H^2/(2n)})
                 \left(c\rho-\frac{\phi(0)D}{6}\right).
\end{equation}
A raw bound and a resource bound have different uses. A raw ceiling
licenses a lower Gaussian density; a resource ceiling bounds $nD$.
The computations below keep them separate at every step. In the two-pass
statement we write the physical resource as $S$ and its numerical ceiling
as $U$.

\paragraph{What is proved here.}
The moment inequalities are proved for actual bounded laws, with both
signs and zero atoms allowed. Their insertion into the retained scalar
Fourier enclosure is checked by exact rational arithmetic on a complete
closed cover. The resulting acquisition remains conditional on the
contact/support premises and the full low- and high-frequency inequalities.
No historical arithmetic success is used as a proof of those inequalities.
The source and execution record accompanies this note.
