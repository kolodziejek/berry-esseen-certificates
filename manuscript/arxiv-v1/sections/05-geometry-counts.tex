\section{One marginal, its physical resource, and its count comparison}
\label{ms:geometry-counts}

The parameters in a numerical relaxation need not describe a probability
law. Soundness requires the opposite direction: every actual law under
consideration must produce one parameter point obeying all the inequalities
used by the certificate. We give that map explicitly. In particular,
conditional moments, support endpoints, count probabilities and the defect
budget below always belong to one common marginal.

\subsection{The sign split and its units}

Fix $P\in\mathcal P$ and put
\[
 p=\Pp(X>0),\quad q=1-p,\quad v=pq,\quad
 m=\E X_+=\E X_->0,\quad B_0=\mathbf1_{\{X>0\}}.
\]
Both $p,q$ are positive; a possible atom at zero is assigned to the
nonpositive class. Define $V=\E[X\mid B_0]$ and $W=X-V$, so that
\[
 V=-m/q\quad(B_0=0),\qquad V=m/p\quad(B_0=1),\qquad
 \theta=\E W^2=1-m^2/v<1.
\]
Write $\theta_-=\E[W^2;B_0=0]$ and $\theta_+=\E[W^2;B_0=1]$.
The physical distance between the two conditional means is
\[
 \ell=m/v=\sqrt{(1-\theta)/v}>0.
\]
Set $U=W/\ell$ and $Y=B_0+U$. Then
\begin{equation}
 X=\ell(Y-p),\qquad \E[U\mid B_0]=0.
\label{ms:bernoulli-coordinates}
\end{equation}
Let $U_0,U_1$ have the two conditional laws, and put
\[
 w_i=\E U_i^2,\quad \mu_i=\E U_i^3,\quad
 s=qw_0+pw_1=\frac{v\theta}{1-\theta},\quad
 \lambda=ns,\quad \ell=(v+s)^{-1/2}.
\]
The moments $\mu_i$ are finite under the original third-moment assumption.
Higher moments used later require bounded support or a separate proved
moment bound. At a selected extremizer, bounded support is available from
Lemma~\ref{ms:contact}; a quantitative support radius remains a further step.

If $s=0$, both nonnegative conditional variances vanish, so $U_0=U_1=0$
almost surely. This is exactly the standardized Bernoulli law. If $s>0$,
the parameter $\alpha=pw_1/s$ lies in $[0,1]$ and gives
$w_0=s(1-\alpha)/q$, $w_1=s\alpha/p$. No division by $s$ is used on the
zero-noise face.

The signs of $X$ are still those defining the two classes, so expansion
of the actual conditional moments gives
\begin{align}
 \rho/\ell^3&=v(1-2v)+3v(w_0+w_1)-q\mu_0+p\mu_1,\notag\\
 \gamma/\ell^3&=v(q-p)+3v(w_1-w_0)+q\mu_0+p\mu_1,\notag\\
 \beta/\ell^2&=v(q-p)+pw_1-qw_0.
\label{ms:conditional-moments}
\end{align}
For example, on the nonpositive class $|X|^3=\ell^3(p-U_0)^3$;
on the positive class it is $\ell^3(q+U_1)^3$. Conditional centering removes
the linear terms and gives the first identity; the other two follow in
the same way. The common pair $(\mu_0,\mu_1)$ must be retained whenever
several such identities are used together.

\subsection{A nonnegative physical defect}

We now suppose the support is compact. Define
\[
 \tau(t)=\E[X\mathbf1_{\{X\ge t\}}],\qquad \sigma=\sqrt{10}.
\]
For $t<0$, centering gives $\tau(t)=-\E[X\mathbf1_{\{X<t\}}]\ge0$;
for $t\ge0$ nonnegativity is immediate. Signwise Tonelli integration gives
\begin{equation}
 \int_\R\tau(t)\,dt=1,
 \qquad 2\int_\R|t|\tau(t)\,dt=\rho.
\label{ms:tau-moments}
\end{equation}
On every bounded component $(a,b)$ of the complement of the closed support,
$\tau$ is constant in the interior; denote that value by $\tau_{a,b}$.
Set
\begin{equation}
 J=\sum_{(a,b)}\tau_{a,b}(b-a)^2,
 \qquad D=(3+\sigma)\rho-\gamma-3J.
\label{ms:physical-defect-definition}
\end{equation}
There are at most countably many gaps. If the support lies in $[-R,R]$,
then $J\le2R\int\tau=2R$, so this sum converges. Endpoint atoms do not
alter the Lebesgue integrals defining its weights.

\begin{lemma}[Physical defect and the central gap]
\label{ms:physical-defect}
The quantity $D$ in \eqref{ms:physical-defect-definition} is nonnegative.
If $0$ belongs to the support, then $D\ge(\sigma-1)\rho$. Consequently
$D<(\sigma-1)\rho$ implies a genuine central support gap $(-A,B)$ with
$A,B>0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If zero belongs to the support, every gap has one sign and
$(b-a)^2\le2\int_a^b|t|\,dt$. Sum using the constant gap weights and
\eqref{ms:tau-moments} to obtain $J\le\rho$. Thus
$D\ge\sigma\rho-\gamma\ge(\sigma-1)\rho$.

Otherwise compactness and the presence of both signs give the central
gap $(-A,B)$. Write
\[
 T_+=\E X_+^3-mB^2,\quad T_-=\E X_-^3-mA^2,
 \quad R_+=\E X_+^2-mB,\quad R_-=\E X_-^2-mA,
\]
all nonnegative. Let $J_o=J-m(A+B)^2$ be the sum over the outer gaps.
For a positive outer gap $(a,b)$,
\[
 (b-a)^2\le2\int_a^b(t-B)\,dt.
\]
Summing and using Tonelli bounds its total contribution by
\[
 J_o^+\le2\int_B^\infty(t-B)\tau(t)\,dt
       =\E[X_+(X_+-B)^2]=T_+-2BR_+\le T_+.
\]
Reflection of this integral calculation, without changing the fixed law,
gives $J_o^-\le T_--2AR_-\le T_-$. Expansion of $D$ yields the identity
\begin{align}
 D={}&m\{\sqrt{\sigma+1}A-\sqrt{\sigma-1}B\}^2
       +(\sigma-1)T_++(\sigma+1)T_-\notag\\
    &+3(T_++T_--J_o).\label{ms:defect-decomposition}
\end{align}
Here $\sqrt{\sigma+1}\sqrt{\sigma-1}=3$. Every term is nonnegative,
which proves the claim.
\end{proof}

Several elementary consequences are useful when initializing a numerical
geometry box. Integrating $(X+A)(X-B)\ge0$ gives $AB\le1$.
Cauchy--Schwarz gives $1/(2\rho)\le m\le1/2$. The outer-gap estimates give
$J\le\rho+2m\le\rho+1$. Since $\sigma-1>2$,
\eqref{ms:defect-decomposition} implies $T_++T_-\le D/2$; if $D<2$,
then
\[
 m(A^2+B^2)=\rho-T_+-T_-\ge1-D/2,
 \qquad A+B\ge\sqrt{2-D}.
\]
These are necessary conditions of the same physical law, rather than
additional definitions of a relaxed defect.

\subsection{One joint budget for endpoint motion and noise}

Let $\bar A=m/q$, $\bar B=m/p$ be the conditional mean magnitudes, and
let $\Delta_A=\bar A-A$, $\Delta_B=\bar B-B$. They are nonnegative.
Define $p_*=(4-\sqrt{10})/2$.

\begin{lemma}[Simultaneous gap inequality]
\label{ms:simultaneous-gap}
For this central gap,
\begin{align}
 D\ge{}&\frac{(6+2\sigma)(1-\theta)^{3/2}}{\sqrt{pq}}(p-p_*)^2
 +6m\{(A+B)(\Delta_A+\Delta_B)+\Delta_A\Delta_B\}\notag\\
 &+3(\sigma+1)B\theta_++3(\sigma+3)A\theta_-.
\label{ms:simultaneous-gap-formula}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The stronger outer-gap bound in the preceding proof gives
\[
 D\ge(\sigma-1)\E X_+^3+(\sigma+1)\E X_-^3
                      -6mAB+6BR_++6AR_-.
\]
Conditional cubic expansion gives
\[
 \E X_+^3=m\bar B^2+
       \E[(X+2\bar B)(X-\bar B)^2;X>0]
       \ge m\bar B^2+3B\theta_+,
\]
and similarly $\E X_-^3\ge m\bar A^2+3A\theta_-$.
Moreover $R_+=\theta_++m\Delta_B$ and
$R_-=\theta_-+m\Delta_A$. Substitution produces the last two terms of
\eqref{ms:simultaneous-gap-formula} and the deterministic expression
\[
 m\{(\sigma-1)\bar B^2+(\sigma+1)\bar A^2-6\bar A\bar B\}
 +6m\{\bar A\bar B-AB+B\Delta_B+A\Delta_A\}.
\]
The second brace equals
$(A+B)(\Delta_A+\Delta_B)+\Delta_A\Delta_B$.
For the first, use $m^2=(1-\theta)pq$ and the exact identity
\[
 (\sigma+1)p^2+(\sigma-1)q^2-6pq=(6+2\sigma)(p-p_*)^2.
\]
This proves the displayed inequality, with one residual paying for all
the nonnegative costs simultaneously.
\end{proof}

In count coordinates the inward displacements are
\[
 a_0=p-A/\ell=\Delta_A/\ell,\quad
 a_1=q-B/\ell=\Delta_B/\ell,
 \quad d_Y=a_0+a_1=(\Delta_A+\Delta_B)/\ell.
\]
Thus $U_0\le a_0$ and $U_1\ge-a_1$. The physical displacement
$\Delta_A+\Delta_B$ and the count displacement $d_Y$ differ by $\ell$.

\begin{theorem}[Weighted joint resource]
\label{ms:joint-resource}
Suppose $n\ge N>0$ and established bounds for this same law give
\[
 nD\le Nd_D,\quad \theta\le\Theta<1,\quad pq\le v_*,\quad
 D\ge b+6mS_*(\Delta_A+\Delta_B)+K\theta,
\]
where $b\ge0$, $S_*>0$, $K>0$ and $v_*>0$. Put $r=d_D-b$.
If $r<0$ there is no such law. If $r=0$, then $d_Y=\lambda=0$.
If $r>0$, define
\[
 d_* =\frac{r}{6(1-\Theta)S_*},\qquad
 \Lambda=\frac{Nv_*r}{K(1-\Theta)}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
 \frac{d_Y}{d_*}+\frac\lambda\Lambda\le1.
\label{ms:joint-plane}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $m\ell=1-\theta$ and
$n\theta=(1-\theta)\lambda/(pq)$,
\begin{align*}
 Nd_D&\ge nD\ge nb+6n(1-\theta)S_*d_Y
                          +K(1-\theta)\lambda/(pq)\\
 &\ge Nb+6N(1-\Theta)S_*d_Y+K(1-\Theta)\lambda/v_*.
\end{align*}
All weakened coefficients multiply nonnegative quantities. Subtracting
$Nb$ proves the impossible and zero cases without division. For $r>0$,
division by $Nr$ proves \eqref{ms:joint-plane}.
\end{proof}

For instance, on a closed probability interval $I$, established endpoint
floors $A\ge A_l>0$, $B\ge B_l>0$ and $\theta\le\Theta$ supply
\[
 S_*=A_l+B_l,\qquad K=\min\{3(\sigma+1)B_l,3(\sigma+3)A_l\},
\]
\[
 b=\frac{(6+2\sigma)(1-\Theta)^{3/2}}{\sqrt{v_*}}
          \operatorname{dist}(p_*,I)^2,
 \qquad v_*\ge\max_{p\in I}p(1-p).
\]
These choices follow from Lemma~\ref{ms:simultaneous-gap} by dropping the
nonnegative product $6m\Delta_A\Delta_B$. Rational lower enclosures for
$b,K,S_*$ and an upper enclosure for $v_*$ preserve the implication.
The preceding theorem assumes the weighted statement $nD\le Nd_D$;
an unweighted $D\le d_D$ cannot be multiplied by $N$ when $n>N$.

On a band $L\le\lambda\le U\le\Lambda$, the joint plane gives
$d_Y\le d_*(1-L/\Lambda)$. Either positive intercept may be enlarged
outward. A separate smaller marginal noise bound does not, by itself,
replace $\Lambda$ in this joint plane. The two intercepts must still come
from a proved simultaneous inequality.

\subsection{The exact count and score identities}

Let $\pi_j=\binom njp^jq^{n-j}$ for $0\le j\le n$, zero otherwise,
and $F_B$ be the CDF of $\operatorname{Bin}(n,p)$. For independent copies
of the marginal, conditioning on the entire sign vector gives independent
conditional residuals. All sign vectors with $j$ positive entries yield
the same sum law
\[
 W_j=U_{1,1}+\cdots+U_{1,j}+U_{0,j+1}+\cdots+U_{0,n}.
\]
It follows from \eqref{ms:bernoulli-coordinates} that at
$x=np+H/\ell=k+u$,
\begin{equation}
 \Pp(S_n\le H)=\sum_{j=0}^n\pi_j\Pp(W_j\le k+u-j).
\label{ms:count-identity}
\end{equation}
For $0\le k\le n$, subtracting $F_B(k)$ gives the exact signed identity
\begin{align}
 Q:={}&\Pp(S_n\le H)-F_B(k)\notag\\
   ={}&-\sum_{i=0}^{k}\pi_{k-i}\Pp(W_{k-i}>u+i)
      +\sum_{i=1}^{n-k}\pi_{k+i}\Pp(W_{k+i}\le u-i).
\label{ms:count-difference}
\end{align}
The outgoing event is strict and the incoming events are closed. Thus an
atom exactly on a count plane has its correct weight.

Choosing a nearest integer gives $|u|\le1/2$, with either integer allowed
at a tie. Restricting to legal counts $0\le k\le n$ requires either
$-1/2\le np+H/\ell\le n+1/2$ or a separate treatment of the remaining
thresholds. Interior-count normalizations further require $1\le k\le n-1$.
None of these range conditions follows merely from the notation.

The own-count variance is
\[
 t=\E W_k^2=(n-k)w_0+kw_1=\lambda+\delta(w_1-w_0),\qquad
 \delta=k-np=H/\ell-u.
\]
Since $|w_1-w_0|\le s/\min(p,q)$, any established bound
$|\delta|\le\Delta$ and $c_v\ge1/\min(p,q)$ gives, for $n\ge N$,
\begin{equation}
 (1-c_v\Delta/N)\lambda\le t\le(1+c_v\Delta/N)\lambda.
\label{ms:own-count-variance}
\end{equation}
A positive lower multiplier is a separate necessary gate when the lower
bound is used in a denominator.

For the comparison with the same Bernoulli count, set
\[
 \rho_B=(1-2v)/\sqrt v,\qquad
 C=\frac{\sqrt n}{\rho}\{\Pp(S_n\le H)-\Phi(H/\sqrt n)\},
\]
\[
 C_B=\frac{\sqrt n}{\rho_B}
       \{F_B(k)-\Phi(\delta/\sqrt{nv})\},
 \qquad
 f(u)=\frac{\Phi((\delta+u)/\sqrt{n(v+s)})-
                    \Phi(\delta/\sqrt{n(v+s)})}{\pi_k}.
\]
For a legal count $\pi_k>0$. Define the exact repair
\[
 \mathcal R=\sqrt n\{\Phi(\delta/\sqrt{nv})-
                    \Phi(\delta/\sqrt{n(v+s)})\}
                         +C(\rho_B-\rho).
\]
Direct subtraction, using $H/\sqrt n=(\delta+u)/\sqrt{n(v+s)}$, yields
\begin{equation}
 \rho_B(C-C_B)=\sqrt n\pi_k\{Q/\pi_k-f(u)\}+\mathcal R.
\label{ms:master-identity}
\end{equation}
This identity is exact; estimates for its two terms must preserve the same
phase, signed threshold and conditional marginal.

\begin{proposition}[Two conditional local witnesses]
\label{ms:local-witnesses}
Let $\lambda>0$, $n\ge N$, and suppose $\sqrt n\pi_k\ge m_0>0$.
The following are sufficient for $\rho_B(C-C_B)<-\varepsilon\lambda$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Bounds $O,T,R_0,\kappa$ satisfy
\[
 \Pp(W_k>u)+f(u)\ge O,\quad
 \sum_{i\ge1}\Pp(W_{k+i}\le u-i)\le T,\quad
 \pi_{k+i}/\pi_k\le R_0,\quad
 \mathcal R\le\kappa\lambda/n,
\]
where the sum and ratio involve legal counts. If $D_0=O-R_0T>0$ and
$m_0D_0-\kappa\lambda/n>\varepsilon\lambda$, the conclusion holds.
\item A proved whole-count estimate gives
\[
 \rho_B(C-C_B)\le\sqrt n\pi_k\lambda B(\lambda)
                               +\lambda I(\lambda)/n.
\]
If $B(\lambda)<0$ and
$m_0B(\lambda)+\max(0,I(\lambda))/N<-\varepsilon$, the conclusion holds.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For the first witness, discard only the outgoing terms with $i>0$ in
\eqref{ms:count-difference}; then $Q/\pi_k-f(u)\le-D_0$.
Use $D_0>0$ before replacing the count mass by its lower bound in
\eqref{ms:master-identity}. For the second, $B<0$ similarly licenses the
mass replacement, and $I/n\le\max(0,I)/N$ holds for either sign of $I$.
Multiplication by $\lambda>0$ gives the asserted strict inequality.
\end{proof}

This proposition proves an implication from valid analytic probability and
repair bounds. It does not itself supply those bounds. In particular,
marked-crossing, Fourier and confinement estimates cannot be hidden in a
numerical acceptance assumption. At $\lambda=0$, the residuals vanish and
the exact Bernoulli law must be treated by a separate all-parameter theorem.
To infer $C<\CE$ from $C<C_B$, one must also supply $C_B\le\CE$ at every
parameter used. No such external theorem is asserted solely by a certificate
hash or by the preceding identities.

For clarity, the zero-noise threshold convention is elementary. At an
attained positive closed maximum for a Bernoulli law, the threshold must
be a binomial atom. Between consecutive atoms, lowering the threshold
to the preceding atom preserves the CDF and strictly improves the normal
subtraction. Above the final atom the same argument applies, while below
the first atom the discrepancy is nonpositive. Thus $u=0$ and $C=C_B$
at such a maximum. This identifies the endpoint to which the separate
Bernoulli theorem is applied; it does not prove that theorem.

\subsection{Whole-count coupling and its curvature term}

The whole-count witness has a useful part which follows directly from the
same conditional marginal. Suppose an acquired gap gives
$U_0\le a_0$, $U_1\ge-a_1$, where $d=a_0+a_1<1/2$, and put $c=1-d$.
Independently realize all $2n$ variables in the definition of $W_j$ above,
and extend $W_j$ constantly for $j<0$ and $j>n$. The increments of
$j+W_j$ are at least $c>1/2$ inside the legal range and equal one outside.
Consequently
\[
 K=\max\{j\in\mathbb Z:j+W_j\le k+u\},\qquad R=K-k
\]
are well defined. An independent binomial selector has the correct law
for each count, so
\begin{equation}
 \Pp\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^nY_i\le k+u\Bigr)=\E F_B(k+R).
\label{ms:whole-count-cutoff}
\end{equation}
Indeed, conditional on the coupled residuals, the closed event for the
selector occurs exactly at counts not exceeding $K$.

\begin{lemma}[Discrete curvature and crossing moments]
\label{ms:crossing-curvature}
Let $\chi=(n+1)p-k<2$ and $m_B=\max_j\pi_j$. For every integer $r$,
\begin{align}
 F_B(k+r)-F_B(k)-r\pi_k
 \le\frac{m_B}{(n+1)v}
 \left\{(\chi-1)_+r_++\chi_+\binom{r_-}{2}
                                      +\binom{r_-}{3}\right\},
\label{ms:discrete-curvature}
\end{align}
where $r_+=\max(r,0)$ and $r_-=\max(-r,0)$. If $|u|\le1/2$ and
$t=\E W_k^2$, then
\[
 \E R_+\le\frac{t}{(c-u)^2},\qquad
 \E\binom{R_-}{2}\le\frac{t}{[c+\min(u,c/3)]^2}.
\]
If additionally $|U_0|,|U_1|\le A_U$, then
\[
 \E\binom{R_-}{3}\le
           \frac{t\sqrt{A_U^2+3t}}{(2c-1/2)^3}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $0\le j\le n$, the binomial difference identity is
\[
 \pi_j-\pi_{j-1}
    =\pi_j\frac{(n+1)p-j}{p(n-j+1)}.
\]
When its numerator is positive, $p(n-j+1)\ge(n+1)v$.
Thus its positive part is bounded by
$m_B((n+1)p-j)_+/((n+1)v)$; zero extension gives the same upper
bound outside the legal range. For $r>0$, double summation gives weights
$r-i+1$ on these differences at $j=k+i$. Since $\chi<2$, only $i=1$
can contribute positively, giving $(\chi-1)_+r$.
For $r=-h<0$, the corresponding weights are $h-1-i$ on the differences
at $j=k-i$, $0\le i\le h-2$. Use $(\chi+i)_+\le\chi_++i$ and
sum the weights to obtain $\chi_+\binom h2+\binom h3$.

On $R=r\ge1$, crossing monotonicity gives $-W_k\ge cr-u$.
Since $(cr-u)^2\ge r(c-u)^2$, this proves the first moment bound.
On $R=-h\le-2$, the count immediately above $K$ is outside the closed
event, giving the strict inequality $W_k>c(h-1)+u$. Put $j=h-1$.
For $u\le c/3$, differentiation shows that
$j(j+1)/(2(cj+u)^2)$ decreases for $j\ge1$: the derivative has the sign
of $j(2u-c)+u\le0$. Its maximum is $1/(c+u)^2$ at $j=1$.
For $u>c/3$, replace the crossing threshold by the smaller one with
$u=c/3$. This proves the second bound.

For the third, use $u\ge-1/2$ and $j\ge2$. The derivative of
$(j^3-j)/(6(cj-1/2)^3)$ has the sign of
$2cj-(3j^2-1)/2<0$, because $c\le1$. Its maximum at $j=2$ is
$(2c-1/2)^{-3}$. Hence
$\binom{R_-}{3}\le(W_k)_+^3/(2c-1/2)^3$.
Independence and centering give
$\E W_k^4\le A_U^2t+3t^2$. Finally,
$\E(W_k)_+^3\le\sqrt{\E W_k^2\,\E W_k^4}
\le t\sqrt{A_U^2+3t}$ by Cauchy--Schwarz.
\end{proof}

Here $\chi=p-H/\ell+u$. Thus an upper bound
$b=p_+-\min(H/\ell)$ yields precisely the phase function
\[
 \frac{(b+u-1)_+}{(c-u)^2}
 +\frac{(b+u)_+}{[c+\min(u,c/3)]^2}.
\]
This explains the curvature supremum treated elsewhere in the article.
The condition $\chi<2$, a positive own-variance multiplier, and any
normalization such as $\sqrt n,m_B\le1$ remain separate gates.
The marked-crossing estimates needed to bound the remaining linear term
$\pi_k\E R$ in \eqref{ms:whole-count-cutoff} are not consequences of this
curvature lemma; their full analytic derivation is a retained obligation.

\subsection{What an exact finite certificate proves}

The following elementary statement isolates the mathematical role of a
pointwise certificate. It applies equally to tensor majorants and to
auxiliary functions with zero expectation under independence.

\begin{theorem}[Common-marginal expectation and score]
\label{ms:certificate-expectation}
Let $K\subset\R$ contain the support of an actual marginal $P$. Let
$f_0,\ldots,f_d$ be integrable functions under $P$, with $f_0=1$, and let
$m_j=\E f_j(X)$. For a finite tensor sum
\[
 T(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=\sum_{\alpha}t_\alpha
                              \prod_{i=1}^n f_{\alpha_i}(x_i),
\]
independence gives
\begin{equation}
 \E T(X_1,\ldots,X_n)=\sum_\alpha t_\alpha\prod_{i=1}^n m_{\alpha_i}.
\label{ms:tensor-expectation}
\end{equation}
If $T\ge\mathbf1_{\{x_1+\cdots+x_n\le H\}}$ on all of $K^n$ and
\[
 \sum_\alpha t_\alpha\prod_i m_{\alpha_i}
         -\Phi(H/\sqrt n)\le a\rho/\sqrt n,
\]
then the positive closed score of this law at this threshold is at most $a$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each product is absolutely integrable, because independence factors its
absolute expectation into the finite quantities $\E|f_j(X)|$.
Fubini and linearity prove \eqref{ms:tensor-expectation}. Integrate the
pointwise majorant and use $\rho>0$ to obtain the score inequality.
All factors in \eqref{ms:tensor-expectation} are moments of the same
marginal; replacing them by moments of unrelated laws is not this theorem.
\end{proof}

For example, a tensor product containing a centered factor has zero
expectation when all other factors concern different independent variables.
Such terms may improve a pointwise majorant without altering its expectation.
Their integrability still has to be proved. Polynomial factors of degree
above three are legitimate for an acquired compactly supported witness,
but are not automatically integrable for every member of $\mathcal P$.

An exact polynomial positivity certificate often uses Bernstein coefficients.
On $[0,1]$, set $B_{j,d}(t)=\binom dj t^j(1-t)^{d-j}$. They are
nonnegative and sum to one. The identity
\[
 t^k=\sum_{j=k}^d\frac{\binom jk}{\binom dk}B_{j,d}(t)
\]
follows from
$\binom dj\binom jk=\binom dk\binom{d-k}{j-k}$ and the binomial theorem.
Applying it in each coordinate proves that a polynomial
$\sum_\alpha a_\alpha t^\alpha$ of coordinate degrees at most $d_i$ has
tensor Bernstein coefficients
\[
 b_\nu=\sum_{\alpha\le\nu}a_\alpha
                      \prod_i\frac{\binom{\nu_i}{\alpha_i}}
                                      {\binom{d_i}{\alpha_i}}.
\]
Thus nonnegative coefficients prove nonnegativity on the whole closed box.
Affine rational changes of coordinates and rational subdivision preserve
exactness. This argument proves the coefficient-to-polynomial implication;
an acceptance program must still verify the correct polynomial identity,
all coefficients, and full domain coverage.

The event plane needs particular care. On the closed side
$\sum x_i\le H$ the required inequality is $T-1\ge0$; on the other side
it is $T\ge0$. A certificate which verifies only $T\ge0$ at a boundary
point has not proved the closed-event majorant there. Sign orthants,
event-plane intersections and endpoint atoms must be included in the
pointwise proof.

\begin{proposition}[Closed-cover soundness]
\label{ms:closed-cover}
Suppose a family of actual laws and thresholds has an analytically proved
map into a covered parameter domain. A finite certificate has finitely
many roots covering that domain and a finite subdivision tree at each
root. Every split retains both closed children. At each leaf, either
a proved necessary condition excludes every actual mapped point in the
leaf, or a valid whole-leaf inequality bounds its positive score by $a$.
Then all actual points in the root domain have positive score at most $a$.
If every leaf instead excludes a putative attained score $C\ge\CE$, no
such attained witness belongs to the root domain.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An actual point at a parent belongs to at least one child, including if it
lies on the common boundary. Induction up each finite tree transfers the
leaf conclusion to its root. The root union transfers it to the complete
mapped domain. In the contrary-score version an actual point cannot lie
in either kind of accepted leaf, giving a contradiction.
\end{proof}

A root may describe a relaxation containing nonrealizable parameters; this
does not harm soundness. A missing root, an unproved feasibility guard or
an unfinished subdivision does. Alternative valid bounds may be minimized
pointwise, but a minimum of convex functions need not be convex: any
vertex or endpoint maximization must be justified for each complete bound
before such a minimum is taken. Likewise, count outgoing lower bounds,
incoming upper bounds and the Gaussian subtraction must refer to the
same phase. Directed arithmetic proves a finite inequality only after
these analytic and coverage requirements have been met.
