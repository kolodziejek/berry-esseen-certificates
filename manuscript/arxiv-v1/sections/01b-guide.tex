\subsection{A guide to the proof}\label{ms:guide}

A possible counterexample must satisfy two very different demands. Its
moments and support must make the error of normal approximation large;
its repeated iid convolution must also put enough mass on one side of a
threshold. The first demand brings the marginal close to two points. The
second is a discrete comparison with a binomial law. The argument below
keeps those two tasks separate, and uses contact to pass between them.

\paragraph{Fix the value we want to prove.}
At a fixed index, maximize \eqref{v9:intro:excess} over standardized laws
and raw thresholds. The target $c=\CE$ remains fixed. At a first failure
above an independently established base through $41$, the predecessor
bound is $C_{n-1}\le c$. Thus the deleted sum can be estimated with the
same coefficient that appears in the extremal problem. No estimate of an
unknown maximizing ratio is needed at this stage.

A maximizing limit needs care because third moments need not be uniformly
integrable. The moment penalty in the objective supplies the missing
inequality. Thresholds are bounded along a maximizing sequence with
nonnegative limiting excess, and the closed-event Portmanteau inequality
handles moving thresholds and atoms. Contamination by a new atom, followed
by centering and scaling, gives contact on the entire closed support.
Its cubic term proves compact support before higher moments are used.

\paragraph{Find one budget for the departures from two points.}
Write $p=\Pp(X>0)$, $q=1-p$ and $m=\E X_+=\E X_-$.
The sign decomposition is exactly
\[
 X=\ell(B_0+U-p),\qquad B_0=\mathbf1_{\{X>0\}},\qquad
 \E(U\mid B_0)=0,\qquad \ell=m/(pq).
\]
The variance of the physical noise $W=\ell U$ is $\theta$; the variance
of the noise in a sum of $n$ Bernoulli-coordinate observations is
$\lambda=n\E U^2$. These are different quantities.

The gap defect $D$ measures both moment asymmetry and the loss of a
single large support gap. Integrating contact against the zero-bias
density gives the exact identity in \eqref{v9:core:conservation}.
Its nonnegative side is
\[
 \mathcal U=(n+H^2)a+\frac{n\phi(0)}6D+n^{3/2}L.
\]
The leakage $L$ is defined using half-open intervals, so an atom at a
contact endpoint has its proper weight. The identity must be applied to
the same marginal throughout; separate maxima for its costs cannot be
added as though they had independent budgets.

\paragraph{Acquire the moment and support bounds in order.}
A first support bound gives finite fourth and sixth moments. Two quadratic
updates and four signed updates then bound the Fourier remainder and
contract the possible moments. A lower bound for $m$ follows from
$D\ge\sqrt{10}\rho-\gamma-6m$; combining it with
$4m^2+\beta^2\le1$ makes the signed updates effective. Each pass uses only
bounds already proved. In particular, a density floor comes from the
preceding raw ceiling, and a new phase price is used only after its own
raw ceiling has been established.

The selected rational calculation retains every actual-law cell and
every closed probability overlap. It gives $p\in[.24,.54]$; an elementary
moment contraction then gives $p\in[.25,.53]$. These intervals are
necessary conditions for a witness, not descriptions of all feasible
laws. The Fourier proof and the finite arithmetic have distinct roles:
the first explains the implication, the second verifies all of its
specified coefficient and coverage gates.

\paragraph{Use contact again to confine the conditional noise.}
On the acquired range $p\in[.40,.53]$, the initial strip is $|U|<.6$.
If $\lambda\le.075$, elementary moment bounds give
$\theta<.0074$, $\ell>1.992$ and $\rho<1.105$.
For the negative support ray set $z=p$; for the positive ray set $z=1-p$.
Both contact bounds reduce to the same quintic on
$u=1.992(z+1/2)\in[1.7928,2.1912]$.
Its third derivative is negative, its first derivative is concave with
positive endpoint values, and its right endpoint is negative. Separate
radial derivative signs extend the boundary contradiction along the
whole rays. This proves $|U|<1/2$ without subdividing this probability
interval. The two rays are rays of one physical law, not a reflection
argument for its CDF.

On $p\in[.41,.42]$, the complementary noise range can also be handled.
The sign of $H$ is retained in the phase resource. Noise and endpoint
motion consume that same resource before the deletion errors are bounded.
Antichain concentration and signed deletion then give the two required
ray inequalities. The finite closed cover has $128$ sign, threshold and
noise boxes: the low-noise lemma handles $16$, necessary conditions
exclude $84$, and both ray tests pass on the remaining $28$.
This completes confinement on that one panel; it does not complete the
count comparison.

\paragraph{Compare the actual convolution with its binomial skeleton.}
At $np+H/\ell=k+u$, conditioning on the sign count gives
\[
 \Pp(S_n\le H)=\sum_{j=0}^n\binom njp^jq^{n-j}
                    \Pp(V_j\le k+u-j),
\]
where $V_j$ is the sum of the actual conditional noises.
Section~\ref{ms:counts} rewrites the excess over the fixed target exactly
as outgoing count mass, incoming count mass and a Gaussian/moment repair.
A sufficiently negative upper bound contradicts nonnegative excess.
The needed error bounds must be proportional to $\lambda$ uniformly down
to zero. The marked-crossing and curvature calculations are given in full
in Appendix~\ref{v9:count:appendix}. Their positivity gates and complete
cover remain to be supplied on the new acquired domain. At zero noise
the law is Bernoulli and is treated directly.

The main text follows this order from fixed-target extremality to
confinement and counts. The appendices give the selected Fourier proof,
the detailed count estimates and their refinements, and a separate
original-law reduction for four summands. The latter concerns the missing
finite base; it is not an input to the confinement theorem. Historical
acquisition schedules are preserved in the repository but are not silently
included among the premises of this argument.
