\section{Certificates and the remaining global implication}\label{ms:certificates}

The analytical results above have two kinds of conclusions. Some are
identities or inequalities for every law in a stated class. Others acquire
numerical bounds after finitely many rational gates have been verified.
Neither kind supplies an omitted hypothesis of the other. We first state
the remaining global obligation, then specify the computational boundary.

\subsection{The fixed-target global implication}

\begin{assumption}[Independent finite base]\label{ms:base41}
For every integer $1\le j\le41$, one has $C_j\le\CE$, by a proof
independent of the later-index exclusion.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Fixed-target local exclusion]\label{ms:local42}
For every integer $n\ge42$ satisfying
$C_{n-1},C_{n-2}\le\CE$, there is no pair maximizing
\eqref{v9:intro:excess} with $c=\CE$ and nonnegative excess.
\end{assumption}

The second assumption is the missing conclusion of the local program,
not a result of this paper. Its predecessor hypotheses are consequences
of a least-failure choice. The new contact entry and the central-panel
confinement use $C_{n-1}\le\CE$; the latter obtains its two-deletion
concentration from antichains, without a bound on $C_{n-2}$.
We leave both predecessor bounds in the final interface so that any later
comparison using both must declare that use explicitly.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional all-index bound]\label{ms:conditional-global}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ms:base41} and~\ref{ms:local42},
\[
 C_n<\CE\quad(n\ge42),\qquad C^*=\CE.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If the strict tail bound fails, choose the least $n\ge42$ with
$C_n\ge\CE$. Both predecessors are at most $\CE$; at $n=42$ they
are the base indices $41$ and $40$. The fixed-target attainment and
equality statements of Section~\ref{v9:core:section} give a maximizing
pair with nonnegative excess. This contradicts
Assumption~\ref{ms:local42}. Together with the base, the strict tail gives
$C^*\le\CE$. The single Bernoulli law in
\eqref{ms:bernoulli-limit} gives the reverse inequality.
\end{proof}

For the sharp global constant alone it is enough to exclude positive
excess under the same predecessor hypotheses. Indeed, choose the least
$n\ge42$ with $C_n>\CE$ and repeat the argument. This yields
$C_n\le\CE$, hence $C^*=\CE$, without a claim of strictness at each
finite index. Equality-inclusive exclusion additionally needs the
zero-excess case, including the strict finite-index Bernoulli endpoint.
Neither version requires an upper bound for an unknown maximizing ratio,
a global positive ceiling, or an astronomically large terminal index.

\subsection{What a finite certificate must establish}

A certificate has three ingredients. Its domain
description contains every actual parameter tuple allowed by the
analytic hypotheses. Its local proof establishes the required inequality
throughout each closed cell. Its coverage proof shows that the cells
exhaust that domain.

The following elementary lemma is the basic tool for tensor certificates.

\begin{lemma}[Common-marginal expectation and score]
\label{ms:certificate-expectation}
Let $K\subset\R$ contain the support of $P\in\mathcal P$. Let
$f_0,\ldots,f_d$ be integrable functions under $P$, with $f_0=1$, and let
$m_j=\E f_j(X)$. For a finite tensor sum
\[
 T(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=\sum_{\alpha}t_\alpha
                              \prod_{i=1}^n f_{\alpha_i}(x_i),
\]
independence gives
\begin{equation}
 \E T(X_1,\ldots,X_n)=\sum_\alpha t_\alpha\prod_{i=1}^n m_{\alpha_i}.
\label{ms:tensor-expectation}
\end{equation}
If $T\ge\mathbf1_{\{x_1+\cdots+x_n\le H\}}$ on all of $K^n$ and
\[
 \sum_\alpha t_\alpha\prod_i m_{\alpha_i}
         -\Phi(H/\sqrt n)\le a\rho/\sqrt n,
\]
then $\Pp(S_n\le H)-\Phi(H/\sqrt n)\le a\rho/\sqrt n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each product is absolutely integrable, because independence factors its
absolute expectation into the finite quantities $\E|f_j(X)|$.
Fubini and linearity prove \eqref{ms:tensor-expectation}. Integrate the
pointwise majorant and use $\rho>0$ to obtain the score inequality.

\end{proof}

For example, a tensor product containing a centered factor has zero
expectation when all other factors concern different independent variables.
Such terms may improve a pointwise majorant without altering its expectation.
Polynomial factors of degree above three require the corresponding moments.
For the extremal laws used here, these are finite because support is compact.

An exact polynomial positivity certificate often uses Bernstein coefficients.
On $[0,1]$, set $B_{j,d}(t)=\binom dj t^j(1-t)^{d-j}$. They are
nonnegative and sum to one. The identity
\[
 t^k=\sum_{j=k}^d\frac{\binom jk}{\binom dk}B_{j,d}(t)
\]
follows from
$\binom dj\binom jk=\binom dk\binom{d-k}{j-k}$ and the binomial theorem.
Applying it in each coordinate proves that a polynomial
$\sum_\alpha a_\alpha t^\alpha$ of coordinate degrees at most $d_i$ has
tensor Bernstein coefficients
\[
 b_\nu=\sum_{\alpha\le\nu}a_\alpha
                      \prod_i\frac{\binom{\nu_i}{\alpha_i}}
                                      {\binom{d_i}{\alpha_i}}.
\]
Thus nonnegative coefficients prove nonnegativity on the whole closed box.
For $P(x)=\sum_{i=0}^d a_i x^i$ on $[l,h]$, the change
$x=l+(h-l)t$ gives power coefficients
$\widetilde a_j=(h-l)^j\sum_{i=j}^d a_i\binom ij l^{i-j}$.
Applying the preceding identity gives the Bernstein coefficients on that
interval. Rational affine changes and subdivision preserve exactness. A certificate must also verify the polynomial identity and the coverage
of the domain.

The event plane needs particular care. On the closed side
$\sum x_i\le H$ the required inequality is $T-1\ge0$; on the other side
it is $T\ge0$. A certificate which verifies only $T\ge0$ at a boundary
point has not proved the closed-event majorant there. Sign orthants,
event-plane intersections and endpoint atoms must be included in the
pointwise proof.

\begin{proposition}[Closed-cover soundness]
\label{ms:closed-cover}
Suppose a family of actual laws and thresholds has an analytically proved
map into a covered parameter domain. A finite certificate has finitely
many roots covering that domain and a finite subdivision tree at each
root. Every split retains both closed children. At each leaf, either
a proved necessary condition excludes every actual mapped point in the
leaf, or a valid whole-leaf inequality bounds its positive score by $a$.
Then all actual points in the root domain have positive score at most $a$.
If every leaf instead excludes a putative attained score $C\ge\CE$, no
such attained witness belongs to the root domain.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An actual point at a parent belongs to at least one child, including if it
lies on the common boundary. Induction up each finite tree transfers the
leaf conclusion to its root. The root union transfers it to the complete
mapped domain. In the second version no actual point can lie in any leaf, a
contradiction.
\end{proof}

A root may describe a relaxation containing parameters of no actual law;
this does not affect soundness, whereas a missing root or an unfinished
subdivision does. A minimum of convex bounds need not be convex, so an
endpoint maximization must be justified for each bound before the minimum
is taken.

In interval computations every transcendental function is enclosed
outwards and every denominator is proved positive on the whole cell; a
lower bound for a binomial mass multiplies a bracket only after the
bracket is known to be negative.

A cover must include both signs of the phase, all admissible nearest
counts, the endpoints of probability panels and the boundaries between
noise bands. The case $\lambda=n\E U^2=0$ is separate: then $U=0$ almost
surely, the law is a two-point law, and Schulz's theorem
applies~\cite{Schulz2016}.

\subsection{Execution and the trusted boundary}

The acceptance programs use exact rational arithmetic and directed
rational enclosures. They compare the complete ordered proof data, reject
duplicate keys and distinguish numerical types. Source hashes identify
inputs; they do not establish the mathematical correctness of those inputs.

An execution audit found that disabling Python bytecode writes does not
prevent reading an existing valid cache. A hostile cache could therefore
alter a phase export while every pinned source hash remained unchanged;
module search order supplied another route to the wrong implementation.
The selected execution path now compiles the explicitly named source bytes
and runs in an isolated child with a fresh cache location. The unchanged
coarse-entry ancestor is copied from its $13$ pinned source and data files into a
fresh private tree and checked before and after execution. The repaired
path reproduces the genuine output and rejects the hostile fixtures.
The Python interpreter, standard library, operating system, rational
arithmetic semantics and the analytic-to-code correspondence remain part
of the trusted boundary. This is not a Lean formalization.

The repository includes the proof objects, exact programs, input hashes,
commands, return codes and separate review reports. Bounded reproduction
uses \path{mathematics/fixed-target-v8/2026-10-28/run_checks.py}; the isolated
coarse-entry replay is
\path{mathematics/fixed-target-v8/2026-10-28/isolated_entry_replay.py}.
The evidence directory is \path{runs/fixed-target-v8-20261028/}.
No native large-scale certificate-generation campaign is part of these
manuscript checks.

\subsection{Which computations have actually been removed}

For the selected coarse entry, the fixed coefficient and signed moment
feedback replace the older unknown-score initialization. Its dependency
chain no longer includes the degree-six phase ancestor, the six intervening
coarse high-frequency updates, or the older twelve-stage signed schedule and its export evaluation.
The comparison is $11264$ signed boxes in the new component against
$239616$ prescribed in the old component, plus their respective separate
initial calculations. It is not a measured speedup or a percentage reduction
of the whole proof. The two quadratic passes, complete Fourier estimates,
actual-law event bounds, and ordered support acquisition remain.

After that entry, the moment contraction is elementary and needs no new
Fourier integration. The continuous low-noise proof replaces the additional
thirteen-panel boundary table by one quintic and its derivative signs; it
also removes the phase table from the lowest-noise part of the completed
central panel. The incoming $.6$ strip is still essential. Larger noise,
other probability panels, the strict count comparison and the independent
finite base are not removed by these results. Existing certificate files
and their original targets are preserved as historical alternatives.

\subsection{What remains to complete the conjecture}

A full local proof must still acquire the required confinement on the
other surviving probability panels at larger noise, then prove the strict
count comparison on every remaining count, phase and noise cell. In
particular, several normalization and cubic-repair formulas in the
appendices assume $p<1/2$, while the acquired range reaches $.53$.
Those assumptions cannot be omitted. Amplitude bounds below $1/2$ must
also be quantitatively strong enough for each selected count denominator.
The equality case and zero-noise comparison must have their stated scope.

Independently, the finite base through $41$ remains open in this work.
Appendix~\ref{r4bridge:section} keeps the original marginal in the
four-summand response and proves the quadrature and threshold-interpolation
reserves. It does not provide all the necessary numerical rows or a complete
four-summand cover. A five-summand result which assumes the four-summand
bound cannot supply that missing parent. No historical success marker or
partial-checkout file list is used to infer otherwise.
