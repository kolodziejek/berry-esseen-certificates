\section{Certificate conditions and the global implication}\label{ms:certificates}

The preceding reductions concern the shape of a proof. To obtain the
sharp inequality from them, the remaining finite statements must be
specified and connected to actual probability laws. This section records
that connection and the present limits of its verification. A checked
polynomial inequality is one part of this connection; it cannot supply
compactness, a contact identity, or the validity of a change of index.

\subsection{The mathematical interface}

Write $a_n=C_n-\CE$ and
$d_n=n(C_{n-1}-C_n)_+$. The symbol $d_n$ here is a scalar loss between
global constants. It is distinct from the geometric inward displacement
and from the nonnegative moment defect $D$.

\begin{assumption}[Finite base]\label{ms:base41}
For every integer $1\le j\le41$, one has $C_j\le\CE$.
The proof of these statements is independent of the tail implication
below.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Equality-inclusive local exclusion]\label{ms:local42}
For every actual integer $n\ge42$, there is no attained positive closed
maximizing pair satisfying simultaneously
\begin{equation}\label{ms:local-entry}
 \begin{gathered}
 0\le n(C_n-\CE)\le0.54,\qquad d_n=0,\qquad C_n<0.4226,\\
 C_{n-1}<0.4253,\qquad C_{n-2}<0.4253.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
This statement includes $C_n=\CE$.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{ms:local42} is an \emph{analytic and computational}
obligation, not a numerical certificate by definition. Its proposed
derivation has four stages. First, the contact inequality and the moment
bounds give the initial support interval. Second, an ordered sequence of
Fourier estimates, moment intersections and support contractions gives
one closed parameter panel containing the maximizing law. Third, a joint
defect inequality and a confinement argument bound its conditional noise
and its support near the two-point skeleton. Fourth, the exact
conditional-count representation gives a comparison on every remaining
closed phase and noise cell. The proofs and hypotheses of these stages
are given in the main text and technical appendices; their inherited
numerical premises have not been replayed in this manuscript project.

The order is part of the assertion. A support contraction may use a
moment bound proved from the previous support interval. It may not use
a moment bound whose proof already assumes the new interval. The law,
the raw threshold and the sign of the discrepancy must stay fixed through
the sequence. A deleted convolution is estimated using a global constant
at the deleted index, but no new maximizing law is substituted into the
current moment or contact identities.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional all-index bound]\label{ms:conditional-global}
Suppose Assumptions~\ref{ms:base41} and~\ref{ms:local42} hold, and use the
fixed-index attainment result of Section~\ref{ms:extremizers} at equality
as well as at strict exceedance. Then
\[
 C_n<\CE\quad(n\ge42),\qquad C^*=\CE.
\]
The finite-base conclusions remain the weak inequalities stated in
Assumption~\ref{ms:base41}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If the strict tail conclusion fails, choose the least $n\ge42$ with
$C_n\ge\CE$. Both predecessors are at most $\CE$: at $n=42$ these are
exactly the base indices $40$ and $41$. Hence $d_n=0$ and the two
deleted caps in~\eqref{ms:local-entry} hold. The support-free recurrence
of Section~\ref{ms:smoothing} gives
\[
 0\le n(C_n-\CE)<\frac{3003}{10250}<0.3,
 \qquad C_n<\frac{73}{175}<0.4226.
\]
Attainment supplies the positive closed maximizing pair. It satisfies
every premise of Assumption~\ref{ms:local42}, a contradiction. The base
and tail give $C^*\le\CE$; the fixed Bernoulli limit
\eqref{ms:bernoulli-limit} gives the reverse inequality.
\end{proof}

If only a local exclusion for $C_n>\CE$ is available, the same proof,
using the first strict exceedance, gives the weak global bound. It does
not justify the strict tail. No eventual two-point optimality or
classification of all extremizers is asserted in either case.

\subsection{What a finite certificate must establish}

An admissible certificate has three distinct ingredients. Its domain
description contains every actual parameter tuple allowed by the
analytic hypotheses. Its local proof establishes the required inequality
throughout each closed cell. Its coverage proof shows that the cells
exhaust that domain. All three are needed even when every coefficient is
an exact rational number.

The following elementary statement isolates the mathematical role of a
pointwise certificate. It applies equally to tensor majorants and to
auxiliary functions with zero expectation under independence.

\begin{theorem}[Common-marginal expectation and score]
\label{ms:certificate-expectation}
Let $K\subset\R$ contain the support of an actual marginal $P$. Let
$f_0,\ldots,f_d$ be integrable functions under $P$, with $f_0=1$, and let
$m_j=\E f_j(X)$. For a finite tensor sum
\[
 T(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=\sum_{\alpha}t_\alpha
                              \prod_{i=1}^n f_{\alpha_i}(x_i),
\]
independence gives
\begin{equation}
 \E T(X_1,\ldots,X_n)=\sum_\alpha t_\alpha\prod_{i=1}^n m_{\alpha_i}.
\label{ms:tensor-expectation}
\end{equation}
If $T\ge\mathbf1_{\{x_1+\cdots+x_n\le H\}}$ on all of $K^n$ and
\[
 \sum_\alpha t_\alpha\prod_i m_{\alpha_i}
         -\Phi(H/\sqrt n)\le a\rho/\sqrt n,
\]
then the positive closed score of this law at this threshold is at most $a$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each product is absolutely integrable, because independence factors its
absolute expectation into the finite quantities $\E|f_j(X)|$.
Fubini and linearity prove \eqref{ms:tensor-expectation}. Integrate the
pointwise majorant and use $\rho>0$ to obtain the score inequality.
All factors in \eqref{ms:tensor-expectation} are moments of the same
marginal; replacing them by moments of unrelated laws is not this theorem.
\end{proof}

For example, a tensor product containing a centered factor has zero
expectation when all other factors concern different independent variables.
Such terms may improve a pointwise majorant without altering its expectation.
Their integrability still has to be proved. Polynomial factors of degree
above three are legitimate for an acquired compactly supported witness,
but are not automatically integrable for every member of $\mathcal P$.

An exact polynomial positivity certificate often uses Bernstein coefficients.
On $[0,1]$, set $B_{j,d}(t)=\binom dj t^j(1-t)^{d-j}$. They are
nonnegative and sum to one. The identity
\[
 t^k=\sum_{j=k}^d\frac{\binom jk}{\binom dk}B_{j,d}(t)
\]
follows from
$\binom dj\binom jk=\binom dk\binom{d-k}{j-k}$ and the binomial theorem.
Applying it in each coordinate proves that a polynomial
$\sum_\alpha a_\alpha t^\alpha$ of coordinate degrees at most $d_i$ has
tensor Bernstein coefficients
\[
 b_\nu=\sum_{\alpha\le\nu}a_\alpha
                      \prod_i\frac{\binom{\nu_i}{\alpha_i}}
                                      {\binom{d_i}{\alpha_i}}.
\]
Thus nonnegative coefficients prove nonnegativity on the whole closed box.
For $P(x)=\sum_{i=0}^d a_i x^i$ on $[l,h]$, the change
$x=l+(h-l)t$ gives power coefficients
$\widetilde a_j=(h-l)^j\sum_{i=j}^d a_i\binom ij l^{i-j}$.
Applying the preceding identity gives the Bernstein coefficients on that
interval. Rational affine changes and subdivision preserve exactness. This argument proves the coefficient-to-polynomial implication;
an acceptance program must still verify the correct polynomial identity,
all coefficients, and full domain coverage.

The event plane needs particular care. On the closed side
$\sum x_i\le H$ the required inequality is $T-1\ge0$; on the other side
it is $T\ge0$. A certificate which verifies only $T\ge0$ at a boundary
point has not proved the closed-event majorant there. Sign orthants,
event-plane intersections and endpoint atoms must be included in the
pointwise proof.

\begin{proposition}[Closed-cover soundness]
\label{ms:closed-cover}
Suppose a family of actual laws and thresholds has an analytically proved
map into a covered parameter domain. A finite certificate has finitely
many roots covering that domain and a finite subdivision tree at each
root. Every split retains both closed children. At each leaf, either
a proved necessary condition excludes every actual mapped point in the
leaf, or a valid whole-leaf inequality bounds its positive score by $a$.
Then all actual points in the root domain have positive score at most $a$.
If every leaf instead excludes a putative attained score $C\ge\CE$, no
such attained witness belongs to the root domain.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An actual point at a parent belongs to at least one child, including if it
lies on the common boundary. Induction up each finite tree transfers the
leaf conclusion to its root. The root union transfers it to the complete
mapped domain. In the contrary-score version an actual point cannot lie
in either kind of accepted leaf, giving a contradiction.
\end{proof}

A root may describe a relaxation containing nonrealizable parameters; this
does not harm soundness. A missing root, an unproved feasibility guard or
an unfinished subdivision does. Alternative valid bounds may be minimized
pointwise, but a minimum of convex functions need not be convex: any
vertex or endpoint maximization must be justified for each complete bound
before such a minimum is taken. Likewise, count outgoing lower bounds,
incoming upper bounds and the Gaussian subtraction must refer to the
same phase. Directed arithmetic proves a finite inequality only after
these analytic and coverage requirements have been met.

For interval computations, every transcendental evaluation must be
enclosed outwards and every denominator must be proved positive on the
entire cell. In the local comparisons, a lower bound for a binomial mass
may multiply an event bracket only after that bracket is known to be
negative. An inverse-index correction can be bounded at a floor $N$
only with the correct sign. Section~\ref{ms:curvature} proves the zero
clamp needed when the improved curvature estimate changes that sign.

Finally, a closed cover includes both signs of the phase, all admissible
nearest counts, endpoints of probability panels and seams between noise
bands. The branch with zero noise is treated separately. If
$\lambda=n\E U^2=0$, then $U=0$ almost surely and the actual law is the
matched two-point law; Schulz's finite-binomial theorem applies
\cite{Schulz2016}. Dividing an estimate by $\lambda$ and then taking a
limit does not prove this branch.

\subsection{Present verification status}

The new scalar, elementary-support, curvature and score-charge arguments
have written derivations and bounded exact checks. Their independent
reviews checked the stated conditional conclusions. The preserved run
records distinguish constructor checks from independent calculations.
For support, the independent calculation reconstructs the boundary
polynomial and checks all 65 invocations and 390 ray signs. For curvature,
a separate piecewise oracle covers 8,505 exact test cases, together with
corrupted variants and false-rule controls. Those tests support the
implementation of the formula; its continuous-domain justification is
the proof in Section~\ref{ms:curvature}. The resource calculation covers
45 left panels and 47 central panels, with independent outward bounds.

The finite base and the remaining native local predicates have not
received a fresh complete replay in this work. In particular, the
complete current assembler for the $40$--$42$ base range, named in the
inherited contract, was unavailable in the inspected source closure.
An older assembly uses tail and coarse-ceiling inputs. It cannot be
silently identified with the proposed independent base. This is an
unresolved dependency, rather than evidence that the new base is false.

Likewise, the literal older local statements retain finite upper limits
or a floor of $47$. The new support proof removes one such obstruction,
and the parameterized argument in Appendix~\ref{ms:uniform-bridge}
explains the required changes. Completion still requires the exact
selected acquisition and comparison predicates, their analytic
hypotheses and a complete new composition. The technical appendices make
the supplied analytic arguments available for review; inclusion in this
draft is not a claim that their entire chain has been independently
verified.
