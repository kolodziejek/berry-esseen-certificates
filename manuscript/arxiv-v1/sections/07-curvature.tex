\subsection{An exact curvature calculation}\label{ms:curvature}

The local comparison contains the supremum of the phase function of
Section~\ref{ms:counts} over an interval. Despite the positive parts and
the truncated denominator, it is attained at an endpoint or at one
explicit interior point.

For real $b$, $1/2<c\le1$ and $[l,h]\subseteq[-1/2,1/2]$, define
\[
 f(u)=\frac{\pos{b+u-1}}{(c-u)^2}
       +\frac{\pos{b+u}}{[c+\min(u,c/3)]^2},\qquad u_*=c-2b.
\]
All denominators are strictly positive. In the conditional-count comparison $c=1-d_L$, where $d_L$ is an upper bound for $d_Y$ on
the current noise band, and
$b=p_+-\min(H/\ell)$, where the minimum uses all four signed endpoint
quotients.

\begin{theorem}[Exact three-candidate supremum]\label{ms:curvature-thm}
The supremum of $f$ over $[l,h]$ is the maximum of $f(l)$, $f(h)$, and
the value $1/[4(c-b)]$ if
\[
 c>b,\qquad l\le u_*\le h,\qquad
 u_*\le c/3,\qquad u_*\le1-b.
\]
An ineligible third value is omitted. The formula includes singleton
intervals, coincident kinks and $c=1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $f=r+g$ for the two summands. On $b+u>1$,
\[
 r''=\frac{2[3(b-1)+u+2c]}{(c-u)^4}>0,
\]
since its bracket is $3(b+u-1)+2(c-u)>0$. Where $u<c/3$ and $b+u>0$,
\[
 g'=\frac{c-2b-u}{(c+u)^3},\qquad
 g''=\frac{2(3b+u-2c)}{(c+u)^4}.
\]
If also $r>0$, then $3b+u-2c>3-2u-2c\ge0$, so $f$ is convex there.
For $u>c/3$, $g$ is convex piecewise linear. At each of
$-b$, $1-b$ and $c/3$, the derivative has a nonnegative jump; simultaneous jumps
add. Thus none is a strict interior maximum.

Where $r=0$, either $g=0$, or below $c/3$ its derivative changes from
positive to negative only at $u_*=c-2b$. Its positive numerator condition
is $c>b$, and $r=0$ there is equivalent to $u_*\le1-b$.
Its value is $1/[4(c-b)]$. Above $c/3$, $g$ is nondecreasing.
These cases locate a global maximum among the stated candidates.
Continuity handles boundaries and collisions; constant segments contribute
a value already attained at an endpoint or a listed maximizer.
\end{proof}

For any subinterval $[a,d]\subseteq[l,h]$ and $u\in[a,d]$, monotonicity
of the numerators and positivity of the denominators give
\[
 f(u)\le \frac{\pos{b+d-1}}{(c-d)^2}
       +\frac{\pos{b+d}}{[c+\min(a,c/3)]^2}.
\]
The exact maximum is therefore no larger than the largest of these bounds
over a subdivision of the interval. For fixed $b$ and $u$, $f$ is nonincreasing in $c$, so a bound computed
with the smallest admissible $c$ remains valid for larger ones.

\subsubsection{The inverse-index correction}
With the exact curvature bound the inverse-index correction may be
negative. Within a fixed positive-noise cell, the bracket and the
correction have the forms
\[
 B(\lambda)=c_{\rm mark}+\frac{q}{a\lambda+G^2}+s\lambda-t,\qquad
 I_{\rm new}(\lambda)=A_0+\frac{A_1}{\lambda},
 \quad q\ge0,\ a>0,\ A_1\ge0.
\]
Only the curvature contribution to $A_0$ decreases. Put
$I_+(\lambda)=\max(0,I_{\rm new}(\lambda))$. For $n\ge N$,
\[
 m_0B(\lambda)+\frac{I_{\rm new}(\lambda)}n
 \le m_0B(\lambda)+\frac{I_+(\lambda)}N.
\]
Both $B$ and the right-hand expression are convex for $\lambda>0$,
including a possible interior zero-clamp kink, where $m_0>0$ is a lower bound for $\sqrt n\,\pi_k$.
Here $c_{\rm mark}$ is constant within the cell.
Endpoint checks thus cover the closed interval. If an earlier correction
$I_{\rm old}\ge I_{\rm new}$ with $I_{\rm old}>0$ satisfies
$m_0B+I_{\rm old}/N<-1/1000$ at an endpoint, then $0\le I_+\le I_{\rm old}$
and the same strict inequality holds with $I_+$. The condition $B<0$ is
still needed to use the lower bound for the binomial mass.
