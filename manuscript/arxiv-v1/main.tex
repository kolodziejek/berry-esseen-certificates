\begin{abstract}
The conjectured optimal constant in the iid Berry--Esseen inequality is
$\CE=(3+\sqrt{10})/(6\sqrt{2\pi})$. We study this upper bound by maximizing
the excess over the fixed target $\CE\E|X|^3$ at a fixed sample size.
This choice gives compact-support contact and an exact conservation
identity in which excess, moment defect and leakage share one nonnegative
budget. An ordered Fourier calculation and elementary moment inequalities
then restrict a possible counterexample at $n\ge42$, under the stated
predecessor bounds, to a neighbourhood of a two-point law. We give the analytical steps of this approach, including the bounds
and coverage conditions needed by its rational certificates.
A continuous contact argument proves half-amplitude confinement for small
conditional noise on the acquired probability range $[.40,.53]$: both
support rays reduce to a single quintic and elementary derivative signs.
A signed resource argument also completes the conditional confinement step
on the panel $[.41,.42]$ for its full acquired noise range.
The exact conditional-count representation describes the comparison still
needed after confinement. A sharp finite base through $41$ and a complete
local exclusion at every later index remain unproved here. The paper is a
self-contained account of the stated reductions, not a proof of the full
conjecture.
\end{abstract}
