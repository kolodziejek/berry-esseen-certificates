\begin{abstract}
The conjectured optimal constant in the iid Berry--Esseen inequality is
$\CE=(3+\sqrt{10})/(6\sqrt{2\pi})$, approached by a fixed two-point law.
We study analytic reductions for proving the matching upper bound at
every sample size. A least contrary index removes the predecessor loss
from the variational contact inequality. A Gaussian smoothing estimate
then gives a short support proof using boundary inequalities and
monotonicity. To analyze laws close to the extremal two-point law, we
combine a nonnegative moment defect, an integrated contact identity and
an exact binomial-mixture representation. Retaining the score excess
sharpens the joint bound on conditional noise and support displacement;
a piecewise derivative calculation replaces a curvature mesh by at most
three candidates. We give the proofs of these reductions, explain their
connection with the longer Fourier and finite-index arguments, and state
the remaining analytic and certificate obligations. The global conclusion
is conditional on an independent sharp base through $41$ and a complete
local exclusion for every $n\ge42$.
\end{abstract}
