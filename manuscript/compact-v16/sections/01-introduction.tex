\section{The problem, results, and proof strategy}\label{ms:introduction}
Let $\mathcal P$ be the laws on $\R$ with mean zero, variance one and
finite third absolute moment $\rho(P)=\E|X|^3$. For independent copies
$X_1,\ldots,X_n$ of $P$, put $S_n=\sum_iX_i$ and
\[
 \Delta_n(P)=\sup_{H\in\R}|\Pp_P(S_n\le H)-\Phi(H/\sqrt n)|,
 \qquad C_n=\sup_{P\in\mathcal P}\frac{\sqrt n\,\Delta_n(P)}{\rho(P)}.
\]
Here $\Phi$ and $\phi$ are the standard normal distribution function
and density. The sharp-constant conjecture is
\begin{equation}\label{ms:conjecture}
 \sup_{n\ge1}C_n=\CE,
 \qquad \CE=\frac{3+\sqrt{10}}{6\sqrt{2\pi}}=0.409732\ldots.
\end{equation}
Throughout, $H$ is a raw sum threshold; terminating decimals in inequalities
are exact rationals. Affine standardization gives the equivalent result
for any mean and positive variance, with third absolute central moment
divided by the cube of the standard deviation.

Berry and Esseen established the classical order $n^{-1/2}$
\cite{Berry1941,Esseen1942,Esseen1945}. Esseen's lower bound
\cite{Esseen1956} explains the proposed constant. If
$p_*=(4-\sqrt{10})/2$, $q_*=1-p_*$ and
$X=(B-p_*)/\sqrt{p_*q_*}$ for $B\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_*)$,
the fixed-law lattice limit gives
\begin{equation}\label{ms:bernoulli-limit}
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sqrt n\Delta_n(P)}{\rho(P)}
 =\frac{3+q_*-p_*}{6\sqrt{2\pi}(p_*^2+q_*^2)}=\CE.
\end{equation}
Indeed $p_*^2+q_*^2=10-3\sqrt{10}$ and
$(3+q_*-p_*)/(p_*^2+q_*^2)=3+\sqrt{10}$.
Every constant below $\CE$ therefore fails for a sufficiently large sum
of this one law. The upper conjecture is associated with Zolotarev
\cite{Zolotarev1966,Zolotarev1967}; Schulz proves the strict finite-index
bound for every nondegenerate Bernoulli law \cite{Schulz2016}.

\paragraph{Fix the target rather than an unknown ratio.}
At a fixed $n$, maximize
\begin{equation}\label{v9:intro:excess}
 a(P,H)=\sqrt n\{\Pp_P(S_n\le H)-\Phi(H/\sqrt n)\}-\CE\rho(P).
\end{equation}
The strategy is a rigidity argument followed by a comparison with a
Bernoulli sum. The moment penalty gives an attaining law; first variation
produces a majorant that touches the conditional distribution function
on its whole closed support. Integrating this contact relation against
the zero-bias density \cite{GoldsteinReinert1997} gives an identity with
nonnegative terms measuring the excess, displacement of the threshold,
and the defect
\[
 D=(3+\sqrt{10})\rho-\gamma-3J,\qquad \gamma=\E X^3.
\]
Here $J$ is the weighted sum of squared support-gap lengths, defined in
\eqref{ms:physical-defect-definition}. The defect vanishes exactly at the
oriented Bernoulli law in \eqref{ms:bernoulli-limit}. An upper bound on
the remainder in the identity therefore forces a hypothetical
nonnegative-excess maximizer close to that law.

For $n\ge700$, direct inequalities give $3/8<p<93/200$, inward
support displacement below $1/250$, and total conditional noise variance
below $7/20$ in count coordinates. Two contact inequalities bound the
outward ends of both conditional supports. A single-count estimate for
noise at most $1/100$ and a five-count estimate above it then give
strictly negative excess. The shared endpoint and zero noise are included.
The comparison theorem itself, Theorem~\ref{short:count:local}, assumes
only its explicit local law domain; it does not assume extremality,
contact, or a predecessor bound.

\paragraph{The finite verification.}
The computational part establishes a coarse localization estimate.
The analysis places an attained nonnegative-excess maximizer, subject
to the sharp bound at the preceding index, in an explicitly bounded
moment region. A certificate records rational bounds for a finite
subdivision of that region. The checking program reconstructs the
subdivision and verifies that each part either contradicts a necessary
condition or satisfies the claimed bounds. The parts cover the whole
region, including their boundaries. Together with the analytical
estimates, this proves in particular
\[
 nD\le32.20665378,\qquad |H|\le1.83605680
\]
for every such maximizer and every actual $n\ge42$.

Finding and checking the certificate serve different purposes. The
exploratory calculation chooses subdivisions and candidate bounds;
verification reconstructs the prescribed inequalities and checks their
enclosures. The retained calculation uses rational arithmetic, with
proved rational bounds for roots, exponential terms, and Gaussian
constants. It controls whole parameter intervals, rather than inferring
an all-law inequality from sampled examples. The analytical reduction
and the coverage argument are parts of the proof; the data file alone
does not replace them.

Subdivision and finite enclosures are established methods of rigorous
numerical analysis; related methods have been formalized in proof
assistants \cite{SolovyevHales2013}. Computational bounding also has
precedents in Berry--Esseen estimates \cite[Section~2]{Shevtsova2011}.
Our certificate format is specific to this calculation. Its Python
verification is not a formalization of the entire argument, and its
generation and checking share mathematical kernels. The later support
and count comparisons have analytical proofs with short rational
checks. Appendix~\ref{revision:reproducibility} identifies the data,
programs, arithmetic conventions, and separate reproduction commands.

\paragraph{The finite boundary.}\label{ms:scope}
The exclusion of an attained extremizer at $n\ge700$ requires the sharp
predecessor bound. A least failure supplies that bound only if all
earlier indices have been controlled. A base through $699$, together
with the complete entry conditions, implies the sharp conjecture;
Section~\ref{short:composition} proves this implication. A base only
through $41$ locates a first failure in $42,\ldots,699$ but does not
rule it out. Those finite bounds remain open in this work.

\paragraph{Context.}
Fourier and structural moment inequalities progressively improved the
universal upper bound \cite{vanBeek1972,Prawitz1972,Tyurin2010,
Shevtsova2011,Shevtsova2013}. We use Shevtsova's structural inequality
explicitly at entry. Recent manuscripts report smaller upper constants
\cite{xiao2026berryesseen,lin2026berryesseen}, and He and Cheng report
eventual sharpness beyond a uniform, very large threshold
\cite{hecheng2026eventually}. Work on independent arrays and extremizers
addresses the related non-iid problem \cite{BellIndependent2026,
BellExtremizers2026}. Tao's registry and the available formalization
projects distinguish numerical certificates, analytical inputs and
formalized implications \cite{tao2026berryesseenregistry,
Yan2026ProbabilityApproximation,BerryEsseenLean2026}. None of these
recent reported bounds is a premise below. An earlier AI-assisted working
draft is on Hexagon \cite{rodziewiczkolodziejek2026momentdefects}.
