\subsection{Reading the argument}\label{ms:guide}\label{ms:notation}
The proof follows one chain: fixed-target maximum, contact and budget,
coarse entry, one support strip, and count comparison. The first step
selects one actual marginal; no later relaxation changes that marginal.
Reflection selects positive closed-event orientation before entry, so
its signed defect is acquired in the orientation where it is used.
The complete Fourier predicates enter only at coarse entry. The later
estimates are analytical, with finite rational inequalities specified
in the proof.

For $B=\ind_{\{X>0\}}$, put $p=\Pp(B=1)$, $q=1-p$, $v=pq$ and
$m=\E X_+=\E X_->0$. If $W=X-\E(X\mid B)$ and $\ell=m/v$, then
\[
 X=\ell(B+U-p),\quad U=W/\ell,\quad \theta=\E W^2,
 \quad s=\E U^2,\quad \lambda=ns=\frac{n\theta}{\ell^2}.
\]
Thus $\theta$ and $\lambda$ use different units; $v+s=\ell^{-2}$.
The inward displacements $a_0,a_1$ are also in count coordinates, and
$d=a_0+a_1$. The physical defect is the distinct symbol
$D=(3+\sqrt{10})\rho-\gamma-3J$, where
$\gamma=\E X^3$, $\beta=\E[X|X|]$ and $J$ is the weighted support-gap
sum. Reconstructing $X$ from $B+U$ leaves the raw threshold $H$ unchanged.

The main text gives the variational, geometric, entry and comparison
arguments. The appendices contain the Fourier enclosure used at entry
and elementary Gaussian constants. Alternative smaller-index and local
routes remain in the repository, outside this selected compilation.
