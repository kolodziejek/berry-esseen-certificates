\section{Discussion}\label{ms:discussion}
Fixing the target aligns the coefficient in the extremal problem,
contact, and the predecessor estimate. Conservation bounds the defect
of a possible extremizer; the equality case explains why the resulting
law is close to the extremal Bernoulli distribution. After coarse
localization, elementary inequalities place that law in one support
region and count conditioning gives the strict comparison.

The small-noise estimate keeps its errors proportional to $\lambda$,
which is essential near zero. The five-count estimate handles
$1/100\le\lambda\le7/20$, where its uniform error is smaller than the
crossing loss. At zero noise the law is Bernoulli and the strict
finite-binomial theorem applies directly.

The argument excludes every attained nonnegative-excess pair at
$n\ge700$ when the sharp predecessor bound and the entry hypotheses
hold. Consequently a proof of $C_j\le\CE$ for all $1\le j\le699$
would complete the global sharp inequality. Those finite bounds are
not supplied here. This work provides neither an independent
four-summand proof nor a complete base through $41$. The retained
computational entry can be replayed separately from the analytical
comparison and its short scalar checks; none proves the missing base.
