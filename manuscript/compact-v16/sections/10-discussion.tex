\section{Discussion}\label{ms:discussion}
The fixed target determines both the third-moment penalty in the
extremal problem and the coefficient in the conservation identity.
The gap inequality explains their common factor $3+\sqrt{10}$.
A bound on the remainder then restricts the defect of a maximizer,
and count conditioning turns that geometric restriction into a strict
comparison with a binomial law.

The number $700$ is a common sufficient floor for two parts of the
proof. The coarse bound satisfies $32.20665378/700<0.047$, as required
by the geometric estimates. The local comparison also uses $n\ge700$
in its binomial-mass and error bounds. Reducing the threshold therefore
requires checking both parts: a sharper localization constant alone
does not establish the comparison at smaller sample sizes. A different
certified localization calculation or a comparison valid on a larger
law region could reduce the remaining finite range, but either would
need its own proof and verification.

The small-noise estimate keeps its errors proportional to $\lambda$.
The five-count estimate has an additive error of order $1/n$, so it is
used on $1/100\le\lambda\le7/20$. At zero noise, the law is Bernoulli
and the strict finite-binomial theorem applies. These three cases give
the exclusion at $n\ge700$ under the hypotheses stated above.
The base cases through $699$ are not treated here; they remain the
additional requirement for the global sharp conclusion.
