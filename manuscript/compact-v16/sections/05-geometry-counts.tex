\section{The sign split and the physical gap defect}
\label{ms:geometry-counts}

The sign split gives two conditional means and two centered conditional
noises. This is an exact description of the marginal; no nearby
Bernoulli law has yet replaced it. The gap defect then measures how much
room remains for these noises and for motion of the support endpoints.

\subsection{Conditional means and the normalization}
For a centered variance-one law with finite third absolute moment, put
\[
 p=\Pp(X>0),\quad q=1-p,\quad v=pq,\quad
 m=\E X_+=\E X_->0,
 \qquad B_0=\ind_{\{X>0\}}.
\]
Both sign classes have positive mass. An atom at zero, if present,
belongs to the nonpositive class. Let $V=\E(X\mid B_0)$ and $W=X-V$.
Conditional centering and the variance decomposition give
\begin{gather*}
 V=-m/q\quad(B_0=0),\qquad V=m/p\quad(B_0=1),\\
 \E(W\mid B_0)=0,\qquad
 \theta=\E W^2=1-m^2/v\in[0,1).
\end{gather*}
Thus $1-\theta$ is the variance carried by the two conditional means.
Their distance is
\[
 \ell=m/v=\sqrt{(1-\theta)/v}>0.
\]
Set $U=W/\ell$. Since $V=\ell(B_0-p)$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{ms:bernoulli-coordinates}
 X=\ell(B_0+U-p),\qquad\E(U\mid B_0)=0.
\end{equation}
Writing $U_i$ for the conditional law of $U$ given $B_0=i$, define
\begin{gather*}
 w_i=\E U_i^2,\quad\mu_i=\E U_i^3,\quad
 s=qw_0+pw_1=\E U^2=\theta/\ell^2,\\
 \lambda=ns,\qquad v+s=\ell^{-2}.
\end{gather*}
In particular $X=(B_0+U-p)/\sqrt{v+s}$. The noise $U$ is measured in
units of the distance $\ell$, whereas $W$ is measured in the original
units of $X$. If $s=0$, both conditional variances vanish and
$U_0=U_1=0$ almost surely: this is precisely the standardized Bernoulli
case. All third moments here are finite. Higher moments will only be
used after support has been acquired.

The signs remain those of the original split. On the nonpositive
class, $|X|^3=\ell^3(p-U_0)^3$; on the positive class it equals
$\ell^3(q+U_1)^3$. Expanding, and cancelling the conditional first
moments, gives
\begin{align}\label{ms:conditional-moments}
 \rho/\ell^3&=v(1-2v)+3v(w_0+w_1)-q\mu_0+p\mu_1,\notag\\
 \gamma/\ell^3&=v(q-p)+3v(w_1-w_0)+q\mu_0+p\mu_1,\notag\\
 \beta/\ell^2&=v(q-p)+pw_1-qw_0.
\end{align}
For example, $qp^3+pq^3=v(1-2v)$ and the quadratic terms are
$3qpw_0+3pqw_1$. All three identities use the same two conditional laws;
their moments are not independent choices.

\subsection{The moment circle and the actual gaps}
The next bounds have no compact-support hypothesis. They will first
control a maximizing law coarsely, before a small central gap is known.
\begin{lemma}[Moment and gap bounds]\label{ms:physical-defect}
\label{ms:fourier:afcore:lem:physical-defect}
For $\sigma=\sqrt{10}$ and the actual $J$ of Section~\ref{v9:core:section},
\begin{gather}\label{ms:gap-family}
 \gamma^2+4m^2\le\rho^2,\quad J\le\rho+2m,\quad
 (2\rho)^{-1}\le m\le\tfrac12,\\
 |\beta|<1,\qquad
 \rho\ge\frac{1+\beta^2}{\sqrt{1-\beta^2}},\qquad
 D:=(3+\sigma)\rho-\gamma-3J\ge0.
 \label{ms:physical-defect-definition}
\end{gather}
If zero belongs to the closed support, $D\ge(\sigma-1)\rho$.
Otherwise its central gap $(-A,B)$ has $A,B>0$, $AB\le1$, and
$J\le\rho+2mAB$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write the actual moments on the two signs as
\[
 a_\pm=\E X_\pm^2=(1\pm\beta)/2,\qquad
 b_\pm=\E X_\pm^3=(\rho\pm\gamma)/2.
\]
All four are positive. Cauchy--Schwarz on each sign gives
\[
 a_\pm^2\le mb_\pm,\qquad m^2\le pa_+,\qquad m^2\le qa_-.
\]
The last two inequalities imply
$m^2(1/a_++1/a_-)\le p+q=1$. Since $a_++a_-=1$, this says
$m^2\le a_+a_-$. Consequently
\[
 b_+b_-\ge\frac{(a_+a_-)^2}{m^2}\ge m^2,
 \qquad \rho^2-\gamma^2=4b_+b_-\ge4m^2.
\]
This is the moment circle. The same inequalities give
\[
 m\rho\ge a_+^2+a_-^2=(1+\beta^2)/2,
 \qquad 2m\le\sqrt{1-\beta^2}.
\]
Here $a_+a_->0$, so $|\beta|<1$ and division is legitimate. Combining
these two bounds proves the stated lower bound for $\rho$ in terms of
$\beta$. Finally $\E|X|=2m\le1$, and another Cauchy inequality gives
$1\le\sqrt{\E|X|\E|X|^3}=\sqrt{2m\rho}$. These prove both bounds for $m$.

For the gap estimate, recall $\tau(t)=\E[X\ind_{\{X\ge t\}}]$.
At negative $t$, centering rewrites it as
$-\E[X\ind_{\{X<t\}}]\ge0$; at nonnegative $t$ its sign is immediate.
Integrating separately over the two half-lines gives
\begin{equation}\label{ms:tau-moments}
 \int_\R\tau(t)dt=1,\qquad
 2\int_\R|t|\tau(t)dt=\rho.
\end{equation}
For a positive gap $(a,b)$,
$(b-a)^2\le b^2-a^2=2\int_a^bt\,dt$; reflection gives the corresponding
negative-gap inequality. A central gap $(-A,B)$ has constant weight $m$
and
\[
 m(A+B)^2=2m\int_{-A}^B|t|dt+2mAB.
\]
Moreover $(X+A)(X-B)\ge0$ almost surely, so
$0\le\E[(X+A)(X-B)]=1-AB$. Summing the gap inequalities against their
constant weights, and using nonnegative Tonelli for countably many gaps,
gives $J\le\rho+2mAB\le\rho+2m$. If zero is in the support there is
no central gap, and the stronger estimate is $J\le\rho$.

Cauchy in $\R^2$, now applied to $(\gamma,2m)$ and $(1,3)$, gives
\[
 \gamma+6m\le\sqrt{10}\sqrt{\gamma^2+4m^2}\le\sigma\rho.
\]
Hence the defect is the sum of two nonnegative losses:
\[
 D=3(\rho+2m-J)+(\sigma\rho-\gamma-6m)\ge0.
\]
In the absence of a central gap, $J\le\rho$ and $\gamma\le\rho$ give
$D\ge(\sigma-1)\rho$. This proves all assertions, including the case
of a zero atom.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Keeping the central and outer costs together}
Suppose the central gap $(-A,B)$ has been acquired. Define
\begin{gather*}
 T_+=\E X_+^3-mB^2,\qquad T_-=\E X_-^3-mA^2,\\
 R_+=\E X_+^2-mB,\qquad R_-=\E X_-^2-mA.
\end{gather*}
These quantities measure excess beyond the two gap endpoints. They are
nonnegative because every positive support point is at least $B$, and
every negative magnitude is at least $A$. Let
$J_o=J-m(A+B)^2=J_o^++J_o^-$ be the outer-gap contribution.
A positive outer gap has $a\ge B$, so
\[
 (b-a)^2\le2\int_a^b(t-B)dt.
\]
Multiplying by its constant gap weight and summing gives
\begin{align*}
 J_o^+&\le2\int_B^\infty(t-B)\tau(t)dt\\
 &=\E[X_+(X_+-B)^2]=T_+-2BR_+\le T_+.
\end{align*}
The negative-side calculation is the same in magnitudes:
$J_o^-\le T_--2AR_-\le T_-$. Endpoint atoms do not change these
Lebesgue integrals; their mass is already included in $\tau$.

Substitute
$\rho=m(A^2+B^2)+T_++T_-$,
$\gamma=m(B^2-A^2)+T_+-T_-$ and
$J=m(A+B)^2+J_o$ into the definition of $D$.
Using $\sqrt{\sigma+1}\sqrt{\sigma-1}=3$ completes the central square:
\begin{equation}\label{ms:defect-decomposition}
\begin{split}
 D={}&m\{\sqrt{\sigma+1}A-\sqrt{\sigma-1}B\}^2
       +(\sigma-1)T_++(\sigma+1)T_-\\
    &+3(T_++T_--J_o).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Every displayed term is nonnegative. In particular
$T_++T_-\le D/(\sigma-1)\le D/2$. If $D<2$, then
\[
 m(A^2+B^2)=\rho-T_+-T_-\ge1-D/2.
\]
Since $m\le1/2$ and $A,B>0$, this gives $A+B\ge\sqrt{2-D}$.
The signed entry estimates retain the full central square rather than
using only this weaker distance bound.

For direct acquisition it is useful to retain the two sharper
outer-gap bounds before dropping $R_\pm$. They give
\[
 D\ge(\sigma-1)\E X_+^3+(\sigma+1)\E X_-^3
                         -6mAB+6BR_++6AR_-.
\]
Replace $J_o$ by $T_++T_--2BR_+-2AR_-$ in the preceding expansion.
Direct acquisition substitutes the conditional means and noises into
this single budget, retaining their joint cost.
