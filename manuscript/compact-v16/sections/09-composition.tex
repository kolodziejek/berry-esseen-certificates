\section{Composition and the finite boundary}\label{short:composition}
We now separate the local exclusion from the induction that could make it
global. The local theorem in Section~\ref{short:count:section} applies to
every marginal satisfying its explicit strip assumptions. Contact,
the predecessor bound and the entry predicates are used to show that an
attained nonnegative-excess witness is one of those marginals.

\begin{theorem}[Acquired-witness exclusion]\label{short:acquired-exclusion}
Let $n\ge700$ and let $(P,H)$ attain the fixed-target maximum at $\CE$
with $a(P,H)\ge0$. Assume $C_{n-1}\le\CE$, the structural inequality
\eqref{v9:entry:structural}, and complete entry
Conditions~\ref{v9:entry:two-pass-condition} and
\ref{v9:entry:signed-condition}. Then such a pair cannot exist.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{v9:entry:theorem} gives
\[
 nD\le32.20665378,\qquad |H|\le1.83605680
\]
for the selected oriented law. Proposition~\ref{short:acq:geometry}
then gives its actual central gap, $3/8<p<93/200$, inward sum
$a_0+a_1<1/250$ and $\lambda<7/20$. The two contact rays of
Proposition~\ref{short:acq:strip} give conditional amplitude $3/5$.
This is where the sharp predecessor enters the support acquisition.

Equation~\eqref{short:acq:normalization} identifies the standardized
Bernoulli-plus-noise marginal with $P$ pointwise. Its threshold is the
same raw $H$, and $1.83605680<46/25$. Thus all hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{short:count:local} hold simultaneously for this marginal.
At positive noise the theorem gives
\[
 a(P,H)<-\lambda/100<0.
\]
At zero noise the marginal is Bernoulli and the strict finite-binomial
theorem gives $a(P,H)<0$ directly. Both cases contradict the assumed
nonnegative excess.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional global conclusion]\label{short:global}
Assume $C_j\le\CE$ for $1\le j\le699$, the stated external structural
and binomial theorems, and the complete entry predicates at every least
failure above this base. Then
\[
 C_n<\CE\quad(n\ge700),\qquad C_n\le\CE\quad(n\ge1),
 \qquad\sup_{n\ge1}C_n=\CE.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose the strict inequality fails at some $n\ge700$, and choose the
least such index $n_0$. The base controls every index below $700$;
minimality controls each index from $700$ to $n_0-1$. In particular
$C_{n_0-1}\le\CE$, including when $n_0=700$.

Failure includes the equality case $C_{n_0}=\CE$. The attainment and
reflection argument in Section~\ref{v9:core:reflection} therefore supplies
an attained positive closed-event pair with $a\ge0$ in either case.
By hypothesis, the full entry predicates hold at this least failure.
The acquired-witness exclusion gives a contradiction. This proves all
strict bounds above the base and hence all weak bounds.

Finally the independent Bernoulli limit~\eqref{ms:bernoulli-limit} shows
that the global supremum is at least $\CE$. Combined with the weak
upper bounds, it gives the claimed equality.
\end{proof}

Under the same entry and external hypotheses, a base only through $41$
has a different consequence: it locates a first
failure, if one exists, in $42,\ldots,699$. An unresolved earlier failure
would prevent us from supplying the sharp predecessor at a later index.
The statement above therefore remains conditional on a genuine finite
base, or on a separate complete argument closing that range.
No unconditional cutoff at $700$ is asserted.
