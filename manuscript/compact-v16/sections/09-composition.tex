\section{Composition and the finite boundary}\label{short:composition}
\begin{theorem}[Exclusion of a nonnegative-excess maximizer]\label{short:acquired-exclusion}
Let $n\ge700$ and let $(P,H)$ attain the fixed-target maximum at $\CE$
with $a(P,H)\ge0$. Suppose $C_{n-1}\le\CE$ and the structural
inequality~\eqref{v9:entry:structural} holds. Then such a pair cannot exist.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{v9:entry:theorem} gives $nD\le32.20665378$ and
$|H|\le1.83605680$ for this same compactly supported law.
Propositions~\ref{short:acq:geometry} and \ref{short:acq:strip} give
the central gap and the following bounds. In
\eqref{revision:canonical-decomposition}, identify the local theorem's
$B$ with $B_0=\ind_{\{X>0\}}$. The variable
$(B+U-p)/\sqrt{pq+s}$ is exactly the original $X$, so the threshold
remains the original raw $H$. The hypotheses match as follows.
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}P{.49\linewidth}P{.44\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
Bound just obtained & Hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{short:count:local} \\
\midrule
$3/8<p<93/200$ & $p\in[3/8,93/200]$ \\
$a_0,a_1\ge0$, $a_0+a_1<1/250$ & Nonnegative inward endpoints, sum at most $1/250$ \\
$-3/5<U_0\le a_0$, $-a_1\le U_1<3/5$ & The stated closed conditional-support bounds \\
$0\le\lambda<7/20$ & Positive-noise interval or separate zero-noise case \\
$|H|\le1.83605680<46/25$ & The full threshold interval \\
Independent copies of $X$ & The iid sum in the local comparison \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Positive noise gives $a(P,H)<-\lambda/100$; zero noise is Bernoulli
and gives strictly negative excess by Schulz. Both contradict $a\ge0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-base implication]\label{short:global}
Assume a genuine finite base $C_j\le\CE$ for $1\le j\le699$,
and use the external structural and strict finite-binomial theorems
cited above.
Then
\[
 C_n<\CE\ (n\ge700),\qquad C_n\le\CE\ (n\ge1),
 \qquad \sup_{n\ge1}C_n=\CE.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $C_n<\CE$ fails above the base, choose the least failing index.
Proposition~\ref{v9:core:attainment} and the reflection argument in
Section~\ref{v9:core:reflection} supply an attained positive closed-event
pair with $a\ge0$. The finite base and minimality give the
predecessor $C_{n-1}\le\CE$. The preceding theorem excludes the pair.
The independent Bernoulli limit~\eqref{ms:bernoulli-limit} gives equality
for the global supremum.
\end{proof}

If only a base through $41$ were known, the same argument would locate
a first failure in $42,\ldots,699$. The coarse theorem would still
localize that maximizer, but the support and count comparisons at
$n\ge700$ would not apply to it. The reflection inequality, including
its endpoint-atom convention, is proved in
Section~\ref{v9:core:reflection}.

\paragraph{Role of finite verification.}
The computational part of the proof consists of
Propositions~\ref{v9:entry:two-pass-condition} and
\ref{v9:entry:signed-condition}, used through
Theorem~\ref{v9:entry:theorem}. The scalar comparisons in
Sections~\ref{short:acq:section}--\ref{short:count:section} are
separate analytical inequalities with short exact checks.
Appendix~\ref{revision:reproducibility} gives the corresponding
reproduction commands. Neither computation supplies the finite base.
