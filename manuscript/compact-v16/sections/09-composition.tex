\section{Composition and the finite boundary}\label{short:composition}
\begin{theorem}[Acquired-witness exclusion]\label{short:acquired-exclusion}
Let $n\ge700$ and let $(P,H)$ attain the fixed-target maximum with
$a\ge0$. Assume $C_{n-1}\le\CE$, the structural inequality
\eqref{v9:entry:structural}, and complete entry
Conditions~\ref{v9:entry:two-pass-condition} and
\ref{v9:entry:signed-condition}. Then such a pair cannot exist.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Entry gives $nD\le32.20665378$ and $|H|\le1.83605680$ for the selected
oriented law. Section~\ref{short:acq:section} gives
$3/8<p<93/200$, inward sum below $1/250$, amplitude $3/5$ and
$\lambda<7/20$. By \eqref{short:acq:normalization}, the standardized
Bernoulli-plus-noise marginal is exactly $P$, with the same raw $H$.
Theorem~\ref{short:count:local} makes $a$ strictly negative, both at
positive noise and at zero, contradicting $a\ge0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional global conclusion]\label{short:global}
Assume $C_j\le\CE$ for $1\le j\le699$, the stated external structural
and binomial theorems, and the complete entry predicates at every least
failure above this base. Then $C_n<\CE$ for $n\ge700$ and
$\sup_{n\ge1}C_n=\CE$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A least failure of the strict bound has a true sharp predecessor by the
base and minimality. Attainment and the closed-event reflection argument
supply its maximizing pair with $a\ge0$; the preceding theorem excludes
it. The independent limit~\eqref{ms:bernoulli-limit} proves sharpness.
\end{proof}
A base only through $41$ instead locates any first failure in
$42,\ldots,699$. An unresolved earlier failure prevents the predecessor
argument at later indices. Thus no unconditional cutoff at $700$ or
complete finite-base proof is asserted.
