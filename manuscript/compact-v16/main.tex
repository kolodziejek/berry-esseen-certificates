\begin{abstract}
We give a conditional reduction of the conjectured sharp iid
Berry--Esseen inequality and prove a strict comparison theorem for an
explicit family of laws close to Bernoulli laws. At a fixed index, the
excess above $\CE=(3+\sqrt{10})/(6\sqrt{2\pi})$ attains its maximum.
First variation and integration against the zero-bias density give a
conservation identity containing a nonnegative defect that vanishes
exactly at the extremal Bernoulli law. Under the sharp bound at the
preceding index and the stated finite-verification conditions, this
identity yields quantitative localization bounds. For $n\ge700$, these
bounds place every hypothetical extremizer in a common probability and
support region. Conditioning on the number of positive summands then
gives strictly negative excess: one count controls small conditional
noise, while five neighboring counts and one concave quartic control
the remaining range. The comparisons include threshold atoms and all
incoming tails. The global sharp inequality follows if the sharp bound
is established for every index through $699$. That finite base is not
proved here.
\end{abstract}
