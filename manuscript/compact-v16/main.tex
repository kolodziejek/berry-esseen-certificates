\begin{abstract}
The conjectured sharp iid Berry--Esseen constant is
$\CE=(3+\sqrt{10})/(6\sqrt{2\pi})$. Using the cited structural and
finite-binomial bounds, we prove that the sharp inequality for every
sample size through $699$ implies the sharp inequality for all sample
sizes, strictly above $699$. The localization step is computer-assisted,
with exact rational verification. The finite base is not proved here.

The argument studies a law and threshold maximizing the excess above
the target $\CE$ at a fixed sample size. The first-order optimality
condition and zero-bias integration give an exact identity involving
a nonnegative support-gap defect. Its lattice specialization is a
moment--span inequality, and its zero case selects Esseen's extremal
two-point law. Exact rational localization, using the sharp bound at
the preceding index, bounds this defect and the threshold. For
$n\ge700$, the resulting law lies in an explicit region around that
two-point law. A direct comparison with a binomial sum then gives
strictly negative excess for every law in this region. This local
comparison is a separate result with explicit support and noise bounds;
it does not require extremality or the predecessor inequality.
\end{abstract}
