## Dependency chain and hypotheses

1. At fixed `n>=2` and positive penalty, attainment and first variation give
   contact on the closed support of an actual standardized law. Compact support
   is proved before using higher moments. Reflection retains its atom inequality.
2. Signwise moment inequalities and support-gap geometry give `D>=0` and its
   equality case, the oriented extremal Bernoulli law. One shared budget charges
   probability displacement, inward gaps and within-sign noise simultaneously.
3. Zero-bias integration of contact gives conservation. Nonnegative excess and
   the strict remainder bound permit the ordered resource bound. The stronger
   arbitrary-law identity retains both residuals in Appendix C.
4. The complete retained coarse-entry conditions, two quadratic passes and four
   signed passes give same-law `nD<=32.20665378` and raw `|H|<=1.83605680`.
   The internal 100-panel cover remains an obligation even though later
   probability-panel refinements are not used.
5. For `n>=700`, scalar geometry gives `3/8<p<93/200`, inward displacement
   below `1/250`, and conditional noise `lambda<7/20`. The genuine sharp
   predecessor plus contact/heat bounds yield amplitude `3/5` on two whole rays.
6. Every law in that explicit strip admits the exact count conditioning.
   For `0<lambda<=1/100`, one own-count quartic and the complete incoming tail
   give strict negative excess. For `1/100<=lambda<=7/20`, five counts, four
   replacements and one concave quartic with two rational endpoints do so.
   The zero-noise case uses the separately cited strict binomial theorem.
   Neither extremality nor a predecessor is a hypothesis of this local theorem.
7. The acquired bounds match the local theorem explicitly. A least-failure
   argument gives the global conclusion only with a genuine base through 699
   and the retained entry assumptions. BASE41 alone leaves 42–699 open.
