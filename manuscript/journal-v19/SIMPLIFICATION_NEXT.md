# What the measured pilots imply for a shorter proof

The delivered manuscript has 98 total pages. Reaching about 60 still needs
roughly 38 pages of genuine reduction. The present work does not demonstrate
that this is impossible, but it does not justify continuing the same
algebra-packaging strategy on the expectation of a large saving.

## Completed and rejected routes

The useful changes are now implemented: one paired Fourier/Pearson argument,
a shorter index-transfer proof, a finite-region table, removal of one
duplicated moment derivation, and a clearer binomial boundary identity.

The proposed signed-coefficient calculus was implemented and tested on a
complete fixed family. It supplied an independent audit, but a full literal
replacement measured 102 pages before and 102 after. The necessary
same-marginal, sign, conditional-variance and shared-budget explanations
outweighed the algebra removed. Do not expand this calculus solely for length
without a new measured example showing a substantial net gain.

The standalone common conditional-moment rewrite also saved no pages.
Those mathematical ideas are already present in the manuscript. Repeating
them at a higher level of abstraction is not by itself a shorter proof.

## A proved common structure, with an explicit limitation

The [conditional-count research note](research/conditional-count/STRUCTURAL_RESULT.md)
places both local witnesses in one exact framework. Couple the actual
conditional noises so that `j+W_j` increases, and let `R` be the last count
crossing the closed threshold. Then

`Q/pi_k = E G(R)`, where `G(r)=(F_B(k+r)-F_B(k))/pi_k`.

The nearest-count and whole-count witnesses are two pointwise majorants of
this same scalar function. The note proves both and preserves strict outgoing
tails, closed incoming tails, exterior counts and every phase sign. Its
binomial degree-elevation identity is now used directly in Section 7.6.

This common identity does not bound the expectation of an arbitrary new
majorant. The nearest proof needs an outgoing quartic bound and the entire
incoming tail sum. The whole-count proof needs neighboring counts, marked
deletion and a fractional-part gain. Those are different probabilistic inputs,
not repeated polynomial expansions that can simply be removed.

Two exact finite-law constructions show that the noise distribution at the
selected count, even together with conditional variances, the first four
ordinary moments and the absolute third moment, does not determine the full
sum CDF. The examples satisfy the indicated normalization conditions but
their scores are below `.4`; they are not acquired attaining laws. Therefore
they rule out only that information-identification shortcut. They do not
refute a useful upper bound, a contact-based argument or the conjecture.

## The next worthwhile mathematical experiment

Try to replace the two expectation arguments by **one estimate for the joint
conditional-count process**. This is the clearest remaining target suggested
by the pilots. The following is a result contract, not an established theorem.

1. Keep the actual iid marginal `Y=B+U` and its two conditional laws. Use their
   acquired inward/outward supports, simultaneous moments, signed threshold
   interval and original joint resource. Do not select unrelated moment
   optimizers separately at different counts.
2. On one central panel and one difficult left panel, construct a finite
   family of piecewise-polynomial majorants `g>=G` and derive an expectation
   estimate under the entire coupled process. The estimate must retain or
   replace the marked-deletion and harmonic information. Checking `g>=G`
   pointwise alone is insufficient.
3. Combine it with the existing exact score identity and signed normalization
   repair. For the uniform zero-allowance comparison, reproduce the current
   strict target `rho_B(C-C_B)<-0.001*lambda` throughout the same domains.
   The bound must hold for every positive `lambda`, with no additive error
   independent of `lambda`. Handle zero noise separately by the Bernoulli
   theorem. Keep finite positive allowances and finite-binomial reserve in
   the exact places where the present proof uses them.
4. Before extending the construction, test both phase signs, exact threshold
   atoms, deterministic conditional components, symmetric and skew Bernoulli
   limits, tiny additional atoms and arbitrarily small positive noise. The
   exact paired-law examples in the research note are additional controls
   against silently identifying different count processes.
5. Acceptance must check complete closed domains and all sign/denominator
   gates using exact arithmetic or documented outward enclosures. Prove the
   same-law expectation rule in the paper. Keep discovery optimization
   separate from acceptance, and do not use sampled positivity as a proof.
6. Only after the two panels work, measure a literal manuscript replacement
   including the soundness proof and examples. Expand to the full finite and
   uniform covers only if it removes substantial analytic duplication while
   improving understanding. Otherwise preserve the current proof.

Even success here might save only part of the missing 38 pages. A larger
reduction could require a new confinement theorem or a propagation theorem
that also eliminates finite-index work. No such theorem is established by
the present one-step recurrence, which has a positive error term. The current
98-page manuscript is the concrete review draft; a 60-page version remains
an open presentation-and-proof task.
