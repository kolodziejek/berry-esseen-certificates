# Direct quartic comparison: revision report

The main change replaces the successive local noise comparisons by a single
comparison with the Bernoulli law having the same sign probabilities. A
coupling expresses all incoming count changes through one conditional sum.
A quartic polynomial bounds the resulting staircase function, and its
expectation is controlled by the first four moments of the same two
conditional laws. A convexity argument reduces the remaining moment and
variance choices to explicit endpoint tests. The proof explains both the
probability argument and why the exact polynomial checks establish it.

For finite indices, an exact binomial calculation supplies an explicit reserve,
with its sign retained in the comparison.
For the central tail, the proof instead displays four quartic inequalities
and preserves a loss proportional to the noise. This distinction matters:
an additive error would not control arbitrarily small perturbations of a
Bernoulli law. The explanation also separates discovery of coefficients
from exact verification of their global bounds and complete parameter cover.

The earlier attainment, contact, support, moment and Fourier arguments remain
part of the proof. The new comparison consumes their original acquired
domains; it does not turn necessary moment constraints into an iid law or
assume a later confinement conclusion. The temporary positive allowance at
index 39 is still proved before the sharp bound at that index. Actual sample
sizes remain distinct from the floors used to acquire their defect budgets.
